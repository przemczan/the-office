# Live view surface: map

**Owns:** the Live view page (`/live-view`, last surface on the rail; [the adopted design](../../../../../docs/design/live-view.html) is its UI spec): The Office drawn as an isometric pixel-art office, each person filling a Role a figure at a desk, drawn in place by their state in the light of the hour where the browser is. `live-view-page.tsx` joins the snapshot with the Roles (`office-people.ts`: `OfficePerson`, the titles and the names), loads the PixiJS stage, holds the nameplates, the zoom buttons, the List toggle and which popover is open. `live-view-types.ts` holds the shapes every part shares, the stage's contract (`OfficeStage`, `StageView`) included.

- **Art** (`art/`): pure grids of palette indexes painted to canvases, cached by shape and palette (`pixel-grid.ts`), the art's palette and the Avatar's tint (`palettes.ts`: `avatarToneIndex` is `Avatar.jsx`'s hash, `toneColours` reads the ramp tokens), the furniture, the floor and walls (`floor-art.ts`, with the slab the room stands on, its shadow and one repeat of the open floor's boards, `BOARDS_REPEAT`, 192 × 96 art pixels), the figures generated from a Role key (`figures.ts`), and the sign, bubble, Z, "+N" tag and glyphs (`overlays.ts`). `CREDITS.md` records the art as The Office's own, GPL-3.0-only, and pixi.js (MIT) as the renderer.
- **The floor plan** (`floor-plan.ts`, pure): groups (front desk, Consultants, everyone else, each in hire order), pods, the floor's size, the entertainment area (a big TV on a low stand with the games console, three floor pillows facing it; it replaced the meeting spot, and with it group meetings went: when two or more ask one Consultant, the first asks at the Consultant's desk and the rest wait at their own), the chill area, the exercise mat (flat, blocking no tile, between the entertainment area and the chill area, its front tile taken first), every pastime's spots (`pastimeSpots`: the pillows, the sofa, the beanbags, the coffee machine, the water cooler, the mat, and in front of each back-wall plant and bookshelf), the door and the tiles furniture blocks; `addHire` and `letGo` change a laid-out floor without moving anyone. `office-geometry.ts` places it in the art's pixels.
- **The hours** (design decisions 11–13; pure): `light.ts`'s `lightAt(now)` is the light: the browser's time zone (`browserZone()`) to a place through `zones.json`, then the phase, the tint (dawn and dusk ramps by the sun's height, white from 14°, steps of 8), the lamps, the glow, the sky in the windows and the sunlight on the floor. `sun.ts` is NOAA's solar equations (sunrise, sunset and civil twilight in minutes after local midnight, polar days and nights, white nights). `zones.json` is generated, never hand-edited: tzdb's `zone1970.tab` and its links (public domain) by `npm run live-view-zones` (`tools/live-view-zones`). A zone it has no place for (`Etc/*`, `UTC`, unknown) gives noon's day light all day. `time-chip.tsx` shows the phase and the clock, with the day's sunrise and sunset in its Tooltip.
- **Pastimes** (`pastimes.ts`, pure; design decision 8): an idle person's time in turns of 4 to 8 minutes from `turnsCountFrom`, each picking from a weighted list (one entry per pastime, games on the pillows included) by the Role key's seed and the turn's index (`seeded.ts`, shared with the art), never the same twice in a row, a nap only from 30 minutes on, and the order of spots to fall back to.
- **States and words** (`live-states.ts`, pure): the look state (asleep only when the turn's pick is a nap, just joined for the first hour, gone home at night), each figure's placement and pose (idle on a free spot of their pick in hire order, else down their own order, else at the desk stretching; two or more on the sofa chat), what each idle person is doing (`Placement.doing`, `doingsOf`, which the stage hands the page as `StageView.doings` for the words), the monitors and the tag, the state in words, each session's line with the engine it runs on (`agentLineWords`: "on Personal", ", standing in for Work" on a Stand-in Engine, whose link in `work-links.tsx` gives why on hover), the links to someone's work (the Decision first) and the canvas's summary with the phase and the local clock; `walksAtLightChange` decides whether a change of light walks people.
- **Walks** (pure, no Pixi): `routes.ts` finds a way round the furniture, four ways along the floor's tiles (`findTiles`, a breadth-first search, null when there is none), and `routeBetween` turns it into points with the step out of a chair and into the next. `walkers.ts` is the state machine of everyone on the floor: `direct` sends each walker where its placement says (the first time, and under the still office, by a jump), `step` walks them at 2.4 tiles a second, and `poseOf`, `depthOf`, `doorOpen`, `visitorsOf`, `conversations` and `sparkles` are what the stage draws from it. `walkers.direct` replaced 35341bed's `moveFigure`.
- **The view** (`view.ts`, `figure-hit.ts`, pure): whole device pixels per art pixel, but for the whole office's exact fit, which may be in-between (`isWholeLevel`, `drawnLevel`) and is the furthest out (`minLevel`); the 80px clamp, zoom round a point, steps and pinch between the whole steps and the fit; the hit test, front-most first.
- **The stage** (`office-stage.ts`, `stage-scene.ts`, `stage-textures.ts`, `stage-input.ts`): the PixiJS application, its sprites sorted by depth, the one ticker (which steps the walkers, moves the walking figures and their nameplates, opens the door and fades the tint), and the canvas's own pointer, wheel, touch and key handling. At an in-between level the world is drawn at the next whole step into a RenderTexture, culled to it, and shown through a sprite scaled down with linear filtering; at a whole step it is drawn straight. The scene's `backdrop` holds the open floor (a `TilingSprite` of the boards a slab lower, reaching 16384 art pixels past the art), the slab's shadow, then the room's floor, slab and walls. The scene is built again whenever a walker sets off, arrives or is gone; in between only walking figures move. `setLight` (given before the first `apply`, then each minute) tints the art, shows the sky sprite over the windows, the sunlight on the floor, desk lamps and the odd pizza box, and how strongly the glows show.
- **The rest of the page:** `time-chip.tsx`, `nameplate.tsx` (nameplates, the `Tooltip`, the `Popover` of someone's work), `people-list.tsx` (the list, a column or, at 844px or less, a `Drawer`), `work-links.tsx` (a `WorkTarget` as a router link), `press-words.ts`, `use-reduced-motion.ts`, `live-view.css`.

**Talks to:** `api/live-view.ts` (`liveViewQueries.snapshot()`, kept whole by `live-view.changed`; only this page reads it, so an Office nobody watches does no extra reads); People's `roleQueries.list()` (Task limit, Consultant, chat, Avatar, `hiredAt`); Work's Task and Initiative lists (titles); the shell's `useClock`, `useNarrowPage`, `PersonAvatar` and page heading; `@the-office/ui`'s `Tooltip`, `Popover` (held open by `open`/`onOpenChange`, so a press on the canvas opens a nameplate's), `Drawer` and `IconButton`.

**Going home** (a5cefa95) uses the walkers as they are: a `gone` placement walks the person out of the door and leaves them hidden with their desk kept; any other placement for a hidden walker walks them back in; and every move through the door in one `direct` (into or out of `gone`, hires, those let go) waits `0.3 + 1.1 × n` seconds in hire order (someone caught mid-walk waits where they stand, `isOnTheMove`, drawn in the walk cycle), so nightfall's "everyone idle goes home" leaves one by one and dawn comes back one by one.

**The fire alarm:** the page reads it from the shell's `nowQueries.now()` (`fireAlarm` not null) and gives it to `apply` as `alarmOn`. Under a softer way (`way` not `asap`) it gives `workGoesOn` too. While it is on, `placementsOf(…, alarmOn, workGoesOn)` puts everyone `gone` (desks kept), but for those working, consulting or waiting on you while work goes on (`STILL_AT_WORK`), the scene shows an untinted alarm light on the left wall just in from the door (`alarmLightShape`, drawn on the wall's face with the iso pen as the door and the windows are, its origin the plate's bottom where it starts along the wall; flashing at 2 Hz, lit steadily in the still office), and `summaryWords` says the alarm is on and everyone is out, or how many are still at work. Sounded and seen live (`walksAtAlarmChange`, the same rule as the light's, plus a visible tab), `direct(…, { alarm: true })` makes everyone on the floor panic on the spot (`walker.panic`, pose `stand-front-panic`, a red "!" over them) for `PANIC_SECONDS`, then run out at `ALARM_SPEED` × the walk, `0.2 + 0.4 × n` apart in hire order; the all clear walks them back in at the door's usual stagger. Unseen, they are put out or back at once.

**Glances and sips** (`glances.ts`, pure): a still figure turns its head now and then (every 6–20 s, 1–2 s), and someone at the coffee machine or the water cooler sips (every 8–15 s, 1.5 s), each once per slot at a seeded point, by the Role key's seed and the wall clock (`Date.now()` handed to `scene.animate`), so two browsers show the same; never in the still office, and never a player's on a floor pillow (`NO_GLANCE`), whose `sit-back-play` profile keeps facing the TV.

**Traps:**

- pixi.js loads only through the page's dynamic `import("./office-stage.ts")`, so it is a chunk of its own and jsdom never loads it: nothing the page imports statically may import pixi.js or the stage files. Page tests mock `office-stage.ts`. Pixi's init asks for WebGL (`preference`), so a browser without it rejects and the page says the floor can't be drawn and opens the list, rather than falling back silently.
- Art colours are numbers (`0xRRGGBB`), Pixi's own form: they are sprite data, an exception to spec 4.1's "tokens only", which holds for everything else on the page, nameplates and list included. The clothes' tint is read from the ramp tokens so it always matches the Avatar.
- Two depth rules from the design: furniture blocks every tile it stands on (the routes fd27cb9e adds need it), and long furniture (the sofa, the long coffee table) sorts by its front-left corner, not its middle.
- Zoom is a whole number of device pixels per art pixel (half only when the office doesn't fit at one), with nearest-neighbour textures, so pixels stay crisp; never set a scale view.ts didn't give. The one exception, the whole office's in-between fit, goes through the RenderTexture: its window into the world starts on a whole texture pixel and the art's origin on a whole device pixel, so each texture pixel always maps to the same place and a pan moves the picture by whole device pixels without shimmering. Positions on the canvas (nameplates, the hit test) use `view.level`, the shown scale, never the drawn step.
- `officeBounds` stays the room: the open floor and the slab's shadow never count for the fit or the pan's clamp.
- The ticker runs only while the page is open and the tab visible, and never under reduced motion, where the still office is drawn with `app.render()` only when the snapshot, the size, the view or the minute changes.
- `lookOf`'s draw order must never change: it is what makes the same Role key draw the same figure everywhere.
- The floor is laid out once per page open; a hire takes a free desk of their group or a new pod at the right end, and nobody else moves while the page is open.
- Routes are searched only in `walkers.direct` and `setFloor`, never per frame, and a frame allocates nothing per walker: `step` answers a reused array, `poseOf` writes into the walker. Only the walkers that moved touch their sprite and nameplate.
- A walk that changes course mid-way starts from the tile underfoot only when it is walkable, else from the next route point's tile, else the last one's: halfway out of a chair a figure stands on the desk's tile, and routing from there would cross the desk.
- Each glow (screen, lamp, LED, TV, exit sign) is an additive sprite sorted in with the items just over what gives it off, never a layer over the scene, so whoever stands or sits in front is drawn over it. A desk's sprites sort by `DESK_DEPTHS` (`live-states.ts`) over the desk's depth: screens, lamp, bulb and pizza, the seat, then the glows just under the sitter, so the one at the desk is drawn over their lamp and its light; the chair's back (unless turned) comes over them.
- Getting up from a seat or sitting down in one, a figure keeps the seat's depth until it is a step away (`depthOf`), so a chair or the sofa never covers it.
- Outside the room (x < 0.1) a figure is drawn in the doorway container, clipped to the doorway and over the open door (whose opening is opaque), and its nameplate is hidden. Nameplates of walking figures move by `onFigureMove` writing `style.transform` straight into the DOM, never through React state.
- The Consultant turns round, and anyone talks, only once the asker has arrived: the scene works out desk activity and conversations from the walkers (`visitorsOf`, `conversations`), not from the snapshot's placement.
- The place comes only from `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`: nothing reads `navigator.geolocation`, nothing asks permission, there is no setting and nothing is sent anywhere (`light.test.ts` spies on the location).
- Untinted on purpose (design decision 2): lit screens, the lamp bulb, the windows' sky, the exit sign, the "+N" tag and the overlays (sign, bubble, Z). Everything else, the cached floor and walls texture and the open floor included, takes `light.tint`; the slab's shadow, a dark translucent smudge, doesn't need it.
- People walk out at nightfall and in at dawn only when the crossing is seen live (`walksAtLightChange`: the previous light under 2 minutes old, and the tab visible, as a hidden tab's clock still ticks). After a hidden tab or a sleeping machine they are put in place, so a whole office doesn't walk at once. Any other change of light (dawn to day, lamps on) walks what the minute changed, as `apply` does.
- Dawn resets the pastime turns: they count from `max(idleSince, light.dawnAt)`, so those back at dawn go to a pastime, never a nap, for 30 minutes first; the asleep words still count from `idleSince`.
- Picks are seeded by the Role key and the turn, never by `Math.random()` or the page's own clock state, so a reload or a second browser shows the same picture at the same minute. A new turn is only a new placement: `walkers.direct` walks people there, pastime changes in one `direct` staggered like the door's.
- The page calls the stage's `apply` only once the snapshot and the Roles have both loaded (`loaded`, null before, apart from an empty office): the first `apply` lays out the floor, so applied with nobody it would lay out an empty floor and then walk everyone in as hires, with sparkles, as happened on coming back after React Query dropped the snapshot (its 5-minute `gcTime`).
- The words for an idle person come from where the floor put them (`StageView.doings`), as their pick may have been full; before the stage reports, or without it, from their pick (`pickedDoingOf`).
- A figure's size comes from its pose (`figureBoundsOf`): the push-up figure lies along the floor, wider and lower than a standing one, and the hit test, the tooltip's anchor and Show on the floor use its bounds. Its box is not centred on its anchor (further on the head's side), which holds only because the mat's spots never mirror it; its anchor is a column toward the head from the body's middle, so the toes stay on the mat's front tile.
- The back view is the same from either way round (nothing of the face shows), so a mirrored back view (facing −y, as the pillows and the spots at the back wall are) reads as facing −x like those at the desks unless the pose says otherwise, and from behind straight on it reads as facing the room's corner: `sit-back-play` is therefore drawn in profile (`paintPlaying`), facing the grid's left so the mirrored seat faces the TV: an eye and the nose on the leading side, the ear and hair behind, the head leaning in, the near knee up in front with the shin down to a shoe, both hands forward on the controller.
- `home` gives a `gone` placement and keeps the desk; a let-go is told apart by the snapshot's keys, never by the placement. A desk nobody sits at has its chair pushed in and its monitors dark.
- Reduced motion remakes the stage (the page's `canvasKey`), so turning it on mid-walk puts everyone in place at once; `walkers.settle()` does the same for a stage that stays.
