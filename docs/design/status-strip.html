The status strip

Scenario

What it proposes

From the user's note on the status strip under the top bar: shorten or squeeze what it shows, two lines rather than one if that helps, a slightly taller strip being fine if it shows more or nothing is cut off on a narrower screen. First Needs you, the engines' usage, desks and tokens; Tasks touched and done since you left go; the Office Manager's Notifications shorter, of a fixed width and always on the right.

  1. Needs you, first and the only filled figure, as today: quiet at 0, tinted while only other Projects have something, accent with a live dot while something waits in the Projects shown. Opens the Needs you panel.
  2. 5-hour window, the engines' usage: the %, a meter that turns amber from 90% and red when used up, the 7-day window and the reset time. Opens Usage by engine.
  3. Desks in use against the Office-wide limit, its dot green while a Task runs.
  4. Tokens today, with the info icon giving each kind, as every token count has.
  5. Workstation: CPU now, and the logical cores where the strip has room (here it hasn't); opens its panel.
  6. The Office's messages: one slot of a fixed width (300px on a wide strip, 260px here) at the right end: the newest with who says it and when, "+3" for the rest, a press listing them all.

1. Two lines: a label over each figure, in a 52px strip

  • Each figure is a label over its value, "Desks in use" over "2 / 3": the words move off the line the number is on, so a figure is as wide as its longer line rather than both together, and four figures take about 430px where today's took about 700px. The label is 11px in --fg-3, the value today's 15px bold number with its small words beside it.
  • The strip grows from 48px to 52px, the 4px the second line needs. Under 600px it takes a second row (section 4).
  • Why labels over values, not one shorter line (the Alternative on the page): one line has to drop words to fit ("2/3 desks" loses "in use", the 7-day window and the meter go first) and still hides parts under 900px; two lines keep every word a figure has at 1,440px down to 900px, and the four figures whole at a phone's width.

2. The four figures first, never hidden

  • On the left, in this order: Needs you, 5-hour window, Desks in use, Tokens today, a hairline between each. At any width they stay; only their extra words go as the strip narrows (section 4).
  • Tasks touched and done since you left go. Since you left keeps its block on Now, where its Outcomes can be read; Tasks touched shows nowhere else, so Now.tasksTouched goes from the read model too.
  • The fire alarm's quiet state goes ("Alarm off · all clear"): the top bar's alarm button already shows it, and in red as "All clear…" while it is on. While the alarm is on, the strip shows it after Tokens today, as a red figure, "Fire alarm" over "On since 14:02" (or "Stopping at safe points"), opening the all clear's review as the top bar's button does: then Desks in use reads 0 and this says why.

3. The 5-hour window, at a glance

  • One engine: "5-hour window · resets 16:40" over "91%, the meter, 7d 41%".
  • Two or three engines: "5-hour window · 3 engines" over each engine's % with a small meter of its own, the engine's name on hover and in the list.
  • Four or more: "5-hour window · 5 engines" over the highest % and "highest".
  • Its colour says how close it is: the % and the meter turn amber from 90% (where "Window nearly used" is raised, WINDOW_NEARLY_USED_PERCENT) and red when the window is used up, the label then saying "used up until 14:20". Today the meter is always grey.
  • A press opens Usage by engine for one engine too, not only for several: each engine's 5-hour and 7-day windows with their meters and reset times. One figure, one behaviour.

4. Narrower strips: the extra words go, never a figure

The strip decides by its own width (a container query), so the rail expanded or the chat drawer beside the page counts as a narrower window would.

The strip's widthWhat changes
1,180px and moreEverything; the slot 300px.
900 – 1,179pxThe slot 260px; the Workstation figure leaves its cores to its panel.
600 – 899pxThe slot 216px. "· resets 16:40" and "7d 41%" go to Usage by engine; "+2 in other Projects" becomes "+2" (its words on hover); the Workstation's label becomes "CPU"; the alarm says "On".
Under 600pxTwo rows, 94px in all. The first holds the four figures alone, spread across, with short labels ("5h window", "Desks", "Tokens") and no meter or hairlines; the second the Workstation, the alarm while on, and the slot filling the rest of the row on one line.

At 390px today's strip shows Needs you, desks and tokens, and loses the usage figure altogether (its .wide class), while a long Office Notification at its start pushes the rest out of sight.

5. The Office's messages: one slot, a fixed width, at the right end

  • One slot for everything The Office tells the user in the strip: its Office Notifications (the Office Manager's, or in the UI's own voice while nobody fills the Role), "Reconnecting to The Office…", "Update scheduled" and the ask to turn on desktop notifications. Today these are four things in four places, the Notification first on the left, where its length moves every figure after it.
  • A short line on top, at most about 34 characters: "Claude Max: 91% of 5-hour window", "Work account is down", "Updated to 1.8.0". Under it, who says it and when, "Pam · 13:05" with Pam's avatar, or only the time while nobody is the Office Manager (spec 2.7: never "System"). The full line, in Pam's words, is in the list.
  • Its left edge and icon take the message's tone, as every block's left edge does in the app: amber for nearly used and reconnecting, red for used up, an engine down and a failed update, green for a reset, a lifted alarm and an update done, blue for an update scheduled, grey for the desktop ask.
  • Which shows: Reconnecting first, then the newest Office Notification, then Update scheduled, then the desktop ask. "+3" counts the rest.
  • A press opens a popover anchored to the slot's right edge, "Office Notifications · 4": every message newest first, its full line, who and when, and its action where it has one (Open Settings › Updates; Turn on desktop notifications). It closes as a popover does: a press elsewhere, Esc or a second press.
  • Fixed width, 300px (260px and 216px on a narrower strip, section 4), so a long message never moves a figure: it is cut with an ellipsis on the slot and whole in the list. With nothing to say the slot is gone, and nothing on the left moves. Only where the figures would otherwise not fit (three engines and the alarm on a strip just over 600px) does the slot give way, down to 168px, so a figure is never pushed out.

The Alternative: one line, 48px

The same order and slot on today's single 48px line, with shorter words ("2/3 desks", "5h 91%"). It is weighed and not recommended: under 1,180px the 7-day window goes, under 900px the meter, the Workstation figure and the words after Needs you go, and under 600px the slot shrinks to an icon with a count, so the narrow strip shows less than the two-line one, and the figures' numbers and words read as one run of text.

This design's names, not glossary terms: a figure, its label and its value, the slot, a short line, the Alternative, and the scenarios. Needs you, Usage window, desk, Workstation figure, Notification, Office Manager and Since you left are used as the glossary and the app have them.

What the build needs

  • A short line for each Office Notification. Notification gains shortLine (packages/contracts/src/notifications.ts), made by the same functions as line in notification.ts (windowNearlyUsed, windowReset, windowUsedUp, engineDown, officeUpdated, officeUpdateFailed and the alarm's), never in a person's voice, as a person shows under it. Other kinds may leave it out until something shows them this short. A migration fills it for open ones from their kind, or the strip falls back to line when it is missing.
  • status-strip.tsx: the figures become one part, StripFigure (a label and a value, as a button or a link), used by Needs you, UsageFigure, desks, tokens, WorkstationFigure and the alarm while on; Figures drops Tasks touched and done since you left; the right end holds the Workstation figure, then the slot. OfficeNotificationsFigure becomes the slot, OfficeMessages, gathering the reconnecting state, the open Office Notifications, ScheduledUpdate and DesktopPermission in the order above, its list a Popover from packages/ui rather than its own portal.
  • usage-figure.tsx: OneEngine opens the list too; the meter's tone from a pure function beside usageFigureWords (usageTone(window): calm, nearly used from 90%, used up), with unit tests.
  • shell.css: the strip's row 52px (grid-template-rows: 56px auto, the strip 52px, or 94px under 600px), container-type: inline-size on the strip and the widths of section 4 as container queries in place of the 720px media query; chat.css's drawer top: 104px becomes the top bar and the strip's heights (a custom property the shell sets, so the two can't drift).
  • The read model: Now.tasksTouched and its counting go from the Notifications module and the contract; doneSinceYouLeft goes from now-words.ts; the Since you left read stays for Now.
  • Spec 4, with the user's agreement: the Shell's status strip becomes "a fixed status strip, each figure a label over its value: needs you, the 5-hour window (…as now…), desks in use and tokens today on the left, never hidden, the fire alarm after them while it is on; on the right the Workstation figure and one slot of a fixed width for what The Office tells the user (its Notifications, reconnecting, a scheduled update, the desktop notifications ask), its newest by a short line, the rest in a list". Tasks touched and done since you left go. Spec 2.7's Today's figures loses "Tasks touched are the Tasks with a Stage run today"; spec 3's Shell line stays. Spec 4.1's chat drawer "sits below the top bar and the status strip" stays true.
  • Tests that move (apps/web/e2e/, each finds the strip as getByRole("status", { name: "Status strip" }), which stays): people.e2e.ts:2921 reads "done since you left", which goes; shell.e2e.ts 468–600 read the meter (.shell-strip-meter) and the Workstation's detail at a narrow width, whose classes and widths change; shell.e2e.ts:721 reads "tokens today", which becomes the label "Tokens today"; updates.e2e.ts:50 reads "Update scheduled", now in the slot. Unit tests: now-words.full.test.ts (doneSinceYouLeft), now-page.test.tsx's tasksTouched. New: the slot's order and count, usageTone, and an e2e test that the four figures stay whole at 390px.

About the mock

  • Its data follows Now, Notification and the usage windows in packages/contracts; the full lines are the service's (notification.ts), in Pam's voice, or the UI's while nobody is the Office Manager. The short lines are this proposal's.
  • Today is drawn from status-strip.tsx and shell.css as built, its 720px rule included. The top bar, rail and Now page around it are as built, in brief.
  • Needs you, the 5-hour window and the slot open what they open (a toast for the Needs you panel and the Workstation panel); hovering a figure gives its words. Like the other pages, it uses the Geist fonts only where they are installed, and makes no network requests.
