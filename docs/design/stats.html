What it proposes

  1. Tokens and cost has two levels. The basic stats are always there, from what The Office already keeps (day_tokens, stage_runs.tokens, task_state_changes, UsageWindowState), with no cost anywhere; the full stats show once Grafana answers, and the range picker and the coverage line come with them.
  2. Drawn by The Office, never embedded panels. Every chart is the design system's bar or line chart over Grafana's data, so it follows the palette, the Project filter and the UI rules. Each has a Tooltip per bar or point on hover and on keyboard focus (one tab stop; the arrow keys step, Shift for ten, Home and End), role="img" with a summary, and Show as a table.
  3. Office-wide says so. Tokens per day, the Usage windows now and the Usage windows over time with their lane carry an "Office-wide" badge; while a Project filter is set, its Tooltip says the filter doesn't narrow them. The filter narrows the top Tasks, Tasks done and Stage retries, tokens and cost over time, and the most expensive.
  4. Cache reads are shown, never counted: a strip of their own under tokens per day, a line in every token Tooltip, a column in the tables.
  5. Estimated cost only in the full stats, always called estimated, priced from Settings › Engines › Model prices.
  6. The states keep the basic stats. The three Basic states say their words in the message at the foot, with the link; Grafana's problems say theirs in an Alert at the top with their next step; a query that fails partway puts Unreachable's words only in the blocks it fed.
  7. Settings › Telemetry gains a Stats group under the export fields, with Your own endpoint picked: Grafana read address, Read token and Test. They apply as soon as they're saved, with no restart; the export fields still apply at the next start, and the actions line says both.
  8. Two tabs, one rail item. Stats' head keeps its title, and packages/ui's Tabs sit under it, as Work's do: Tokens and cost (/stats, everything above, unchanged) and System health (/stats/system-health). Each tab's range picker sits at the right of the tabs' row, as a control that serves one tab does (4.1). The top bar's crumb names the tab.
  9. System health: what The Office's own performance looks like, and what slows it down. Load now (sessions running, Stage runs running, browser connections open), then Responsiveness (event loop lag, p99 and max, with a 100 ms guide), CPU and memory (The Office's CPU against one core, its memory in use and heap used, the workstation's CPU and memory), Requests (requests a minute, p95 time, the slowest routes) and Database (queries a minute, p95 time, connections waiting, the slowest queries). Every block is Office-wide and says so.
  10. Find the cause, not just the symptom. The slowest routes and queries are sorted by total time, what kept The Office busiest, with each one's share; every figure column is a sort, so the one slow outlier (Max) or the slowest on average (p95) is a press away. Routes are named by their template, never the address; a query by its statement shape, values and parameters made ?. Hover the spike at 13:42 in the mock, then read the tables.
  11. 1 h is always there. It comes from The Office's own Health readings, one every 5 seconds, kept in memory for an hour, telemetry On or Off, and refreshes every 5 seconds. 24 h, 7 d and 30 d come from Grafana and show its states in the same words and links as Tokens and cost; with no Grafana to read, they say why and offer Show the last hour. Load now shows on every range.
  12. Every chart has its Tooltip and Show as a table, one table per block with every figure of its charts; a range's steps are 5 seconds (1 h), 5 minutes (24 h), an hour (7 d) and 6 hours (30 d).

Open for the Gate

  • Load now comes first on System health rather than last: it is the one block that is the same on every range, and it says what is running while the charts below say how it went.
  • The Office's CPU is measured against one core rather than the whole workstation: The Office's own work runs on one thread, so near 100% means it queues up, which a share of 16 cores would hide. Its Tooltip gives the share of the workstation too.
  • The slowest tables show 10 rows and Show all for the rest, rather than every row.
  • The lane of what was running is Office-wide, as the windows above it are, so the Project filter doesn't narrow it. It could instead fade the other Projects' rows.
  • 7 d is Tokens and cost's default range (System health's is 1 h): long enough to show the week's rhythm and the 7-day window's reset, short enough that the lane stays readable.
  • Engines without Usage windows are left out of both windows blocks rather than listed with "No Usage windows".

The mock's data is made up and drawn from a seeded generator, the same on every load; today is Tue 17 Nov 2026, 14:05. Like the other pages, it uses the Geist fonts only where they are installed, and makes no network requests. The address keeps every switch and the page's own choices.
