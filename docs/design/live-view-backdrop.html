The space round the room

The Live view draws the office as a room on an empty floor. Two things to fix: the whole-office zoom leaves room above and below the office on a wide screen, and the empty page round the room makes a bright room look cut out on the Dark theme, and a dark room at night look cut out on the Light theme.

1. The whole-office zoom fills the floor Decided It fits exactly (e.g. 367%), drawn at the next whole step and scaled down smoothly. Drawn below for reference.
2. The backdrop blends the room into the page To choose Five backdrops, each round the real room, by day and night on the Light and Dark themes.

Every room here is painted by the Live view's own art (the floor plan, furniture, figures, tint, sky and lamps of art/, ported into this page) for an office of ten, 16 × 9 tiles: the real room, not a picture of it.

The whole-office zoom Decided

A floor of 2560 × 1100 CSS pixels at 1 device pixel per CSS pixel, today's room, no backdrop yet. Today Fit drops to the whole step below; fitting exactly fills the floor inside the same margins (56 above, 64 below, 16 at the sides).

A

Glow

A soft glow in the room's own colour, in the hour's light, fading into the page's background.

How it follows the hour

The glow is the room's mean colour under the hour's tint: cream by day, peach at golden hour, deep blue at night with a little lamp warmth at its heart. setLight tints it with the art, so it fades as the tint fades.

What it costs to draw

One radial gradient texture made once (256 px), one sprite under the floor in the backdrop container, tinted. Nothing per frame.

Under pan and zoom

It is in the world, so it moves and scales with the room. It is the one smooth thing on a pixel floor; zoomed in, only its middle shows, and panning far shows the plain page past it.

VerdictCheapest, and it softens the cut-out look on Dark by day and Light by night. By day on Light it is barely there, and the room still floats on an empty page.

B

Diorama

The room stands on a floor slab, its two front sides showing, with a soft drop shadow under it.

How it follows the hour

The slab's sides are art and take the tint with the floor. The shadow is the theme's, not the hour's: it doesn't change from day to night.

What it costs to draw

The slab is two faces painted into the floor's art (floor-art.ts); the shadow is one blurred texture made once per theme. Nothing per frame.

Under pan and zoom

Moves and scales with the room; the slab is art pixels, crisp at every whole step. Past the shadow the page is as empty as today.

VerdictIt frames the room rather than blending it in: on Dark the shadow disappears into the page and the slab makes the bright room stand out more. Reads as a toy model on a table.

C

Sky backdrop

The hour's sky, the windows' own colours, across the whole floor, with a vignette into the page's background at the edges.

How it follows the hour

The strongest sense of time: SKY_PALETTES' blue by day, gold at golden hour, violet and orange at dusk, navy with stars at night. It changes when light.sky does.

What it costs to draw

A gradient and a vignette the size of the canvas, drawn again on a resize and when the sky changes (a few times a day). Nothing per frame.

Under pan and zoom

It is the sky, so it stays put while the room moves over it: it lives in the stage's screen layer, not in the backdrop container, and the room reads as floating.

VerdictLoud. The theme's palette is gone from the floor: a navy block on Light at night, a bright blue one on Dark by day. A room in the sky also argues with the windows, which show the same sky.

D

Floor everywhere

The office's floor boards run on to the canvas's edges; only the room's two walls stay, like partitions on an open floor.

How it follows the hour

The boards are art and take the tint with the room, so the whole floor darkens together at night and warms at dusk.

What it costs to draw

One TilingSprite of one repeat of the boards (3 × 2 tiles of 32 × 16), tinted, in the world under the room. Endless, one more quad per frame.

Under pan and zoom

Pixel-exact with the room's own boards at every zoom; a pan never finds an edge. officeBounds stays the room, so Fit and the pan clamp don't change.

VerdictBlends the room with the floor, but the floor itself is now a beige box with a hard edge against the shell: on Dark that's a big bright panel, and at night on Light a big dark one. The room loses its outline.

E

Floor everywhere + fog of war Recommended

As D, but the boards fade away from the room into the page's own background: mist on the Light theme, darkness on the Dark one. The fade is stepped and dithered by art pixel, as in a pixel-art game.

How it follows the hour

The boards take the tint with the room. The fog is the page's --surface-sunken, so the floor fades out into the page at every hour and on every palette; only a theme change repaints its colour.

What it costs to draw

The boards painted 9 tiles past the room into the floor's art, and one fog mask made once at art scale, white, tinted with the token. Nothing per frame; both are made again only when a hire widens the floor.

Under pan and zoom

In the world, crisp at whole steps: the fog's steps are art pixels too. A pan shows the floor fading off, never an edge; past the fog the canvas is clear and the page shows.

VerdictThe room sits on its own floor, and the floor dissolves into whatever the page is. No edge on any theme, any palette or any hour. On Light at night, the dark floor fades into a light mist, which reads as fog rather than night.

Try it

One floor, any backdrop, any hour. Drag to pan, scroll or use the buttons to zoom: the whole-office zoom is exact, and the steps in and out from it are whole.

Recommendation: E, floor everywhere + fog of war

  • It removes the edge everywhere. The fog is the page's own --surface-sunken, so the floor meets the page with no line on Light, Dark or any of the other palettes, by day and by night.
  • It keeps the art's language. The boards are the room's own 32 × 16 tiles, and the fog steps by art pixel with a 4 × 4 ordered dither: crisp at whole steps, even at the exact fit, never a smooth blur on a pixel floor.
  • The room stays the room. Its walls, rugs and carpets mark it, the fog starts at its edge, and behind the walls the floor shows only a tile or two before the fog takes it.
  • It is bounded. Unlike D, the floor needs to reach only where the fog is whole (about 6 tiles out, painted to 9), so it is a fixed texture, not an endless one. Fit and the pan clamp keep officeBounds, the room.
  • To build: the boards past the room in art/floor-art.ts (office-geometry.ts grows the art by the apron); the fog mask as a sprite in stage-scene.ts's backdrop container, over the outer boards and under the room's; its tint read from --surface-sunken on the floor element, again when the theme changes.
  • If E is too much: A, the glow, is the cheapest step up from today, and the two combine (a glow under the fog) should night on the Light theme feel too misty.
