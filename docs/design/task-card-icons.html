Chosen: Foot row

The user chose Foot row at the Gate on 2026-11-13; the build follows it. Inline and Corner badges stay on this page as the layouts that were weighed.

The title keeps the card's whole width on its own line, as today, and every other mark sits in one row under it, always in the same order: the Project, then what needs you (Failed, Waiting on you, Paused), then what holds it (Blocked, Queued), then the quiet ones (Planning, the Usage estimate), and at the right end who is working, or Plan in Backlog. Reading down a column, the same mark is always in the same place, so a red or amber mark or a lock count stands out at a glance. Its marks sit away from the title, so a press on a mark rarely lands on the title by mistake, and the foot already exists in BoardCard, so it is the smallest change. A card is two lines unless a long title wraps; only a card holding nearly every mark at once, in a 220px column, wraps its row, and who is working stays at the right end of the second line.

Inline is the densest, but its marks move with the title's last word, so nothing lines up from card to card, and small marks inside a line of link text are easy to miss with a finger. Corner badges reads urgency fastest, but its bubbles sit on the card's edge, over the gap between two cards: they need more space between cards, a bubble can be cut by the column's edge when the board scrolls, and several bubbles on a card in a 220px column crowd the menu.

The marks

Two kinds of hidden information

Plain information (the Usage estimate, what holds a Paused Task, a state, Planning, the Project, the priority, who is working): only the mark shows; its words show in Tooltip on hover and on keyboard focus. With the mouse, a press on such a mark opens the Task, as a press on the card does.

Information with links (the blockers, each a link to its Task; the queue's waits and the Task it is queued behind): the mark is a button, bordered and with a count, and a press opens a popover anchored to it, with the full information and its links. Hovering it first shows a tooltip: "Blocked by 3 Tasks: press for them"; the clock's names its place and the queue's short reasons, as today's card shows them, and the press gives the full ones.

The popover

  • Opens on a press of its mark, or Enter or Space on it; keyboard focus moves to its first link, and Tab walks its links, then on past the mark.
  • Closes on a press anywhere else (which then does what it does), on Esc (focus goes back to its mark), on a second press of its mark, or on following a link.
  • One is open at a time: opening another closes the first.
  • Never starts a drag: pressing a mark turns the card's dragging off until the press ends, and the popover lies on the page's body, outside the card. Nor does it open the Task.
  • Not a window in spec 4.1's sense: no head, no bar, no backdrop, nothing to answer; Esc closes it first, as it does a menu.

By touch

There is no hover on a touch screen, so a press on any mark shows what its tooltip would, in the same popover, and the rest of the card opens the Task. Switch Input to Touch in the bar to try it with a mouse.

Counts

A count shows where it says something at a glance: how many Tasks block it (the lock), its place in the queue (the clock, "#2"), and how many more people work on it than the three avatars shown ("+1"). No count on a state: there is only one.

About the mock

Its data follows Task and QueuedTask in packages/contracts, and its words are the app's: taskStateWords, usageEstimateWords, planningNoteWords, the service's queue reasons. The icons are Lucide, as packages/ui's Icon draws them. Tooltips, popovers, opening a Task (a toast here), Plan, dragging a card within its column and folding a group work. It uses the Geist fonts only where they are installed, and makes no network requests.
