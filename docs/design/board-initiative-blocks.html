Recommended: Proposed, each Initiative gathered into one block per column

With Group by Initiative off, each column gathers its cards of one Initiative into one block, headed by the Initiative's name (a link to its page), how many of its cards the column holds and its Paused badge when it is paused. The block sits where its first card falls in the column's order; inside it, the cards keep that order. Tasks with no Initiative stay loose, each at its own place. The card is the adopted Foot row card, unchanged. The blocks are always there with the switch off, don't fold and carry no colour; they step aside while the Initiative filter is set.

Switch Layout to compare with Today and with Adjacent runs, the alternative for the order; Block mark shows the hue that was weighed, and Drop elsewhere what a drop over another block would do if it were placed rather than refused.

1. The order

Gathered: all of an Initiative's cards in a column make one block, placed where its first card would be; inside it, the column's order (priority, then manual order). A loose card keeps its own place between the blocks.

One block per Initiative per column is what “wrapped in a block” asks for: each Initiative reads once in a column, and the top of a column still holds its first card by priority. The cost: a card further down joins its block higher up, so between blocks the order holds by their first cards only, and a block moves when its first card changes (a drag to the end of its block, a new priority). Adjacent runs keeps the exact order, but one Initiative can show as two or three blocks in a column (Backlog, Planning and Queued here), and blocks split and merge as cards move. A fixed order of blocks (newest Initiative first, as the grouped view) was weighed too: steady under a drag and level across columns, but a High priority card with no Initiative would sink under every block, and lining Initiatives up across columns is what Group by Initiative is for.

Queued and Done

Queued gathers by the same rule over the queue's order: a block sits at its first card's place, its cards by place. As the adopted card shows no place, the block's head in Queued shows its cards' places, “#1 · #3”, where other columns show a count, so the queue's order still reads across blocks.

Done gathers over its own order: a block sits where its card that ended last falls, its cards the one that ended last on top.

Each column keeps the one rule, applied to its own order, so there is nothing new to learn per column; Queued alone needs the places said, because gathering moves a card away from its neighbours in the queue and the card can't tell it.

2. Tasks with no Initiative

Loose cards, outside any block, each at its own place in the column's order. No “No Initiative” block.

A block says “these belong together”, and Tasks with no Initiative don't. On a board with few Initiatives, a No Initiative block would wrap most cards in every column, a frame that says nothing. The grouped view needs a No Initiative group because there every card must sit in a row; here the column already holds them.

3. A block of one, and the Initiative filter

A block of one card is still a block (Backlog and Queued have several).

The frame is how a card says it belongs to an Initiative: a lone card without one would read as a Task with no Initiative.

With the Initiative filter set, no blocks: the columns are flat, as today.

Every card is then that Initiative's, and the filter's select names it: six frames with the same name, one per column, would add a level and say nothing.

4. Drag and drop

  • A card drags within its column, inside its own block; a loose card among the loose cards, landing before the next loose card under the pointer, between blocks too. While it is dragged, its block is outlined and the rest of the column steps back.
  • Dropped among another priority inside its block, it takes that priority, as today. If it was the block's first card, the block then moves to where its new first card falls.
  • A drop over another Initiative's block is refused, with the grouped view's toast in this design's word: “A card moves only within its own block: the board doesn't change a Task's Initiative.” A loose card dropped on a block: “A card with no Initiative moves only among the cards outside the blocks: …”.

Placed instead, the card would take its place among another Initiative's cards in the order and then show back in its own block: the drop seems to do nothing, or moves the card's block, and can change its priority unseen. Try Drop elsewhere: Placed.

5. Folding, and when blocks show

Blocks don't fold, and they are always on with the switch off: no switch of their own.

A block is a part of one column: a fold per block would be up to six folds per Initiative, each hiding two or three cards. Folding a whole Initiative is the grouped view's, and showing one Initiative alone the Initiative filter's. A second switch beside Group by Initiative would be two near-identical layouts to tell apart; the user asked for the board to read this way.

6. Telling blocks apart

No colour. The head's name, the frame and its fill tell a block from the next one and from loose cards. To follow one Initiative across columns, hovering or focusing a block's head outlines its Initiative's blocks in every column (scenario One Initiative across).

Initiatives have no colour (it would be new data, or a hue picked from the id that means nothing), and the palettes' hues are taken: teal and violet are Projects' marks, amber, blue, green and red are states. A card already carries two colours, its state's edge and its Project's mark; a third on its block reads as one more of those. Switch Block mark to Hue to see the clash.

The block's head

  • The flag (the icon the app uses for an Initiative, in mentions and on a Task's page) and the name, a link to the Initiative's page, wrapping however long it is.
  • The Paused badge (InitiativePausedBadge, small) while the Initiative is paused.
  • At the end, how many of its cards the column shows, with the filters; in Queued their places. Its tooltip says it in words.

No state badge, Projects or progress: those are the grouped view's header, read once per Initiative; repeated in every column they would outweigh the cards.

About the mock

Its data follows Task, QueuedTask and Initiative in packages/contracts: four Initiatives across the-office, not-astray and notnullsoft, one with a long name, Tasks with no Initiative, and cards in every column. The search matches titles only, standing in for the service's. Dragging works within a column, as the board's: a card that has not started, Backlog to Ready. Opening a Task or an Initiative, a mark's popover, Plan, the Task menu and Group by Initiative show a toast. It uses the Geist fonts only where they are installed, and makes no network requests.
