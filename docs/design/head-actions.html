Head actions The buttons and the More menu at the top right of a Task and an Initiative: three ways to tidy them.

What gets in the way today

The two busiest heads, as the app draws them now. An active Initiative can offer ten actions at once, and a stopped Task five. The numbers mark the problems listed under them. Resize the browser window: the row hides its actions from the end, so what you see depends on the width.

    Three options

    All three keep the design system’s Button, SplitButton and Menu, and the four tones. They differ in how far they go: A only merges actions that are alike, B also gives each kind of action a fixed place, and C moves the actions out of the head into a bar of their own.

    B’s zones

    B sorts every head action into one of three zones, always in this order, so each kind is always in the same place. A zone with nothing to offer takes no room, and its divider goes with it.

    Task page head

    The states with the most actions, in each option. Click a split button’s arrow or More to open its menu; hover an icon to see its words.

    Initiative page head

    The Initiative has the longest row. In B and C, Add a Task and Add an existing Task move to the head of the Tasks block, under the head, where the Tasks they add show up.

    More menu

    Today More lists every head action first, then its own items, so an active Initiative’s menu runs to 13 items and repeats the ten buttons beside it. In B and C, More holds only what the head doesn’t show: its own items, in groups, plus whatever the width hides.

    B on a narrow page

    At 900px and below the actions go under the title, as today. B then folds the Go to zone into More, under the heading Go to, and shows Hold and stop and the secondary step as icons in their tone, with their words on hover. The primary keeps its words.

    Notes for the review

    Recommendation: B, zones with one next step

    • It answers each complaint at its root: no tone shows twice in a head, the switch is gone from the row, links read as links, and the primary is always last before More.
    • The busiest head drops from ten buttons to four controls and two icons, so nothing is hidden at a usual width, and the row no longer changes with the window.
    • It stays where the eye already looks (top right) and costs no height, unlike C.
    • It is mostly a change to RecordActions and the hooks that build the actions; no new design-system part is needed.
    • C’s bar is worth a later look if you want each state to say why its buttons are there, but it overlaps the Needs you card and the Start notes, so I would not start there.

    Choices B makes that you may want to change

    • One primary. When an active Initiative offers both Plan the Initiative and Start Ready Tasks, Start Ready Tasks is the primary (work that waits only on you) and Plan the Initiative is the secondary. Today Plan the Initiative is the primary.
    • Resume the Initiative becomes the primary while the Initiative is paused, as Resume is on a Task.
    • Auto mode becomes a choice in Plan the Initiative’s menu (On or Off, with a check), and the button shows a small “Auto” tag while it is on. The remembered default stays as it is.
    • Restart from… and Start over move into Resume’s menu: they are the other ways a paused Task goes on.
    • Back to Shaping moves to More: it is rare and undoes a step, so it shouldn’t sit among the buttons that move work on.
    • More stops repeating the head’s actions. It lists only those the width hides, under Go to on a narrow page.

    What the build changes, for B

    • RecordAction gets a zone (go-to, hold, next) in place of the order the hooks push today; RecordActions draws the zones with their dividers.
    • An action can carry alternatives (menu items), which draws it as a SplitButton; a go-to action is an IconButton with its words in a Tooltip.
    • withActionItems adds only the hidden actions to More, and the hide-from-the-end logic gives way to the narrow rule: the go-to zone into More, the others as icons.
    • The Initiative page’s Tasks block head gets Add a Task as a split button, with Add an existing Task in its menu.
    • Spec: screens/task-page.md (Head actions, Task menu, Run controls), screens/initiative-page.md (Head actions, Initiative menu, Tasks block), 4.1-ui-rules.md (Action tones: one of each tone per head, go-to actions have none).
    • The Idea card uses the same RecordActions at the small size, so B’s rules carry over to it; it isn’t drawn here.

    For the Gate

    Say which option to build, and anything to change in it. When you choose B, also say whether you are happy with:

    1. Start Ready Tasks as the primary over Plan the Initiative.
    2. Add a Task and Add an existing Task in the Tasks block, not in the head.
    3. More holding only what the head doesn’t show.
    4. Auto mode as a choice in Plan the Initiative’s menu, with the “Auto” tag on the button.
