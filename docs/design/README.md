## The Initiatives tab: what it proposes

Round 2, after the user's note of 2026-11-09 on round 1 (sections by state on one page): just adding grouping isn't enough; the tab should be closer to the Tasks tab's design, and it must hold hundreds or thousands of Initiatives after months or years. So it borrows the two things that keep the Tasks tab readable however much work passes through it: a **board** by state, and the **Archive**. One recommendation for each question, with why.

1. **Grouping by state: a board, as the Tasks tab has.** The tab is the Work board's parts re-used: its filter bar, its columns (a sunken column with a head, a count and a hint) and its cards (one link, a coloured edge, a foot of small facts). Three columns, left to right as an Initiative moves: **Shaping** ("With Michael, until you confirm them"), **Active** ("Their Tasks are on the Work board") and **Done** ("Done and dropped in the last 30 days, then archived"). **Dropped** sits folded at the foot of the Done column, as the Work board's Done column holds Done and Dropped, but never mixed with them: no two states share a list, and the Dropped ones are two columns and a fold away from the Active ones. At 390px the columns stack in the same order.
   A board rather than sections on one page (round 1) or a **List** with a State filter kept in the address (`?state=`, the alternative in the mock): the board is what the user already reads on the Tasks tab, shows every live state at once, and stays one screen long because the Archive takes what ended. The List shows one state at a time, denser, but hides Shaping behind a chip while the user looks at Active, and looks like nothing else on Work but the Archived tab.
2. **Keeping it short for years: the Archive, extended to Initiatives.** A Done or Dropped Initiative leaves the board **once it has ended for the Archive period** (Settings › Office limits, 30 days, the one Tasks use), or at once by **Archive** in its card's More menu. Archived isn't a state: it stays Done or Dropped, its page and Threads stay readable, and **Restore** puts it back in Done for a full period, as for a Task. Archived Initiatives show in the **Archived** tab, beside the archived Tasks, with a `SegmentedControl` between Tasks and Initiatives (`?kind=initiatives`): a search of names and outcomes, a Done / Dropped / All filter with counts, the one that ended last on top under a line per month, **25 to a page** with `Pagination`, and the address keeping the search, the filter and the page. After two years (the mock's "Two years in": about 1,260 Initiatives) the board still holds only what moves and what just ended, about 30 cards, and the other 1,230 are a search away.
3. **The order inside each group:**
   - **Shaping** and **Active:** newest first, by `createdAt`, as `GET /initiatives` already answers. Initiatives have no priority or manual order, and an order by what moves would shift cards under the pointer as Tasks change state; the edges and the Waiting on you chip find what needs the user instead. A manual order, dragged as Tasks are, can come later if the user asks; it isn't proposed.
   - **Done**, its **Dropped** fold and the **archive:** the one that ended last on top. That needs **one new field**, `endedAt`, the time it became Done or Dropped: the Archive period counts from it too, so the build needs it anyway.
4. **Each card:**
   - **Active:** the **name** (the card's one link, wrapping however long it is); its **Projects**; its **outcome**, two lines at most, or "Outcome not written yet."; **progress**, a small `ProgressBar` and `progressWords` ("2 of 7 Tasks done · 1 ready to start"); and a foot with **what moves now**, each only when there is one: "2 waiting on you" (a warning `Badge`), "1 in progress" (In progress or Paused, with the live dot), "1 blocked" (not started, waiting for a Task that isn't Done or Dropped), and at its end the avatars of who is working on its Tasks now, as a Task card's working mark. Its **edge** follows the Task card's colours: amber while a Task waits on the user, green while one is in progress, none otherwise. An Active Initiative with nothing to count says so instead of "0 of 0 Tasks done": "No Task left: every one was dropped. Add a Task or drop the Initiative on its page.", or "No Task yet. Add one on its page."
   - **Shaping:** the name, its Projects (or "No Project yet"), its outcome, and a foot "Shaping with Michael · 2 Tasks so far" (or "No Task yet"), as a Planning card says "Planning with Angela". No bar: none of its Tasks can be done before Confirm.
   - **Done or Dropped:** a compact card: the name; a foot with its Projects, its final count ("3 of 3 Tasks done", "No Task done") and when it ended ("Done 3 Nov", "Dropped 20 Oct"); a green edge for Done, red for Dropped; and a **More** menu (Open, Archive), as `TaskMenu` on a Task card.
   - **Widths:** three columns on a wide page and at 900px; at 390px they stack, the outcome goes from the cards (it is on the Initiative's page), the search takes a line of its own and the fold's "Latest" goes. The archive's table becomes a list of rows, the name on a line of its own and the state, date and Restore under it. Nothing scrolls sideways.
5. **Finding one:** the filter bar has a **search** of names and outcomes and a **Waiting on you** chip (Initiatives with a Task waiting on the user, with its count), and Clear while either is set; both are kept in the address (`?query=`, `?waiting=1`), as the Work board's filters are. While the search matches archived Initiatives, a line under the bar says so ("2 archived Initiatives match “sync” too.") with **Search the archive**, which opens the archive on the same words. No Saved views: a Saved view is the Work board's (glossary), and two filters don't need one.
6. **Folding:** only **Dropped** folds, at the foot of Done, folded at first; folded, its line shows its count and the latest dropped ("Latest: Slack notifications, 30 Oct"). Its fold is **remembered in this browser**, in local storage as `board-group-folds.ts` keeps the Work board's groups, never in the address: someone looking back over what was dropped goes in and out of Initiative pages and shouldn't unfold it again each time. Shaping, Active and Done don't fold: the Archive keeps them short.
7. **Counts and empty states:**
   - Each column's head counts the cards it shows, the filters and the Project filter applied; the Dropped fold counts its own; the Waiting on you chip counts its matches; the archive's filter chips count theirs, and `Pagination` says "1–25 of 1,230".
   - **Every column always shows**, so the board keeps its shape; with no card, its dashed empty line says what would fill it ("Nothing being shaped. Shape an Idea into an Initiative from Ideas.", "Nothing Active. Confirm an Initiative in Shaping to start its Tasks.", "Nothing done in the last 30 days."), or why it shows none: "None matches the filters." or "None in the Projects shown." **Dropped leaves the column** with none to show: an empty fold is noise.
   - **The Project filter** narrows the board and the archive; an Initiative with no Project yet always shows, as today. The note "7 more Initiatives on the board in other Projects" with Show all projects stays above the board, and the archive has its own. The foot of Done counts the archived Initiatives in the Projects shown, with **Open the archive**.
   - **No Initiatives at all:** today's empty card, "No Initiatives yet. Shape an Idea into one from Ideas." An empty archive says how an Initiative gets there.

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: Today, Proposed: Board and Alternative: List (the layouts compared); the Initiatives board, a column and its hint, a card's edge and what moves now; the Dropped fold and its Latest; Open the archive, Search the archive and the archive's month lines; and the scenarios Every state, Dropped unfolded, Search: sync, Waiting on you, Nothing Shaping, The archive, Two years in and No Initiatives. "Waiting on you", "In progress", "Paused" and "blocked" on a card are the Task states and the Blocked filter as the Work board names them. **Archive**, **Archived**, **Restore** and **Archive period** are glossary terms, for a Task today; extending them to Initiatives is a glossary change (below).

### What the build needs that the app doesn't have

- **The glossary and [spec 2.1](../spec/2.1-work.md), with the user's agreement:** Archive, Archived, Restore and the Archive period extended from Tasks to Initiatives ("Taking a Done or Dropped Task or Initiative off the board…"); spec 2.1's Archive line the same, Initiatives that ended longer ago than the period archived at the first run, as Tasks were; and beside Group by Initiative: "The Initiatives tab is a board: Shaping, Active and Done, each newest first but Done, the one that ended last on top, with Dropped folded at its foot; its search and Waiting on you are kept in the address; the Dropped fold is remembered in this browser." The click-through's `initiativesList` changes with it.
- **A contract change:** `endedAt` and `archivedAt` on `Initiative` in `packages/contracts/src/work.ts`, each `z.iso.datetime().nullable()`, as `Task.archivedAt`; the archive's answer (`GET /initiatives/archived?query=&state=&page=`, 25 to a page) with its total and each Initiative's final count, so the archive never loads every Initiative and every Task; and `initiative.archived` and `initiative.restored` among the web events.
- **A service change:** the two columns, with their migration. `endedAt` is set where the Initiative moves: to Done in `finishInitiative`, and by Confirm when it makes one Done at once; to Dropped in `dropInitiative`. The migration fills `endedAt` in from `task_state_changes` where a Task's move dates the end (the latest move of a Done one's Tasks to Done or Dropped; when a Shaping one's Tasks were dropped with it); one nothing dates (dropped while Active, which changes no Task) is archived at the first run, with no date. The daily run that archives Tasks archives Initiatives by the same period, from `endedAt` or the last Restore; `POST /initiatives/:id/archive` and `/restore`; `GET /initiatives` answers the Initiatives on the board, and its `?query=` searches names and outcomes. "What moves now" and progress on the board keep coming from `GET /tasks`, which the Tasks tab already loads.
- **`packages/ui`:** `DataTable` scrolls sideways on a narrow page, which the archive can't (spec 4.1's Narrow rule), so it gains a stacked look under 640px (each row a block), which the Archived Tasks table would take too. `SegmentedControl`, `Pagination`, `Badge`, `ProgressBar` and `FoldButton` are used as they are.
- **In the web app:** `initiatives-list.tsx` becomes the board, re-using the Work board's column and card styles from `work.css` rather than its own; a filter bar for it (search and Waiting on you, in the address) made as `filter-bar.tsx`'s first row; an Initiative's More menu, as `task-menu.tsx`; the Dropped fold's memory, a small store made as `board-group-folds.ts`'s (its own storage key); the Archived tab's Tasks / Initiatives switch, its crumb "Archived"; and on the Initiative page an Archived badge with Restore and Archive in its More menu, as on the Task page (`archive-parts.tsx`).
- **A test moves:** `apps/web/e2e/people.e2e.ts` (line 4586) finds the Active card as a link by its name and reads "0 of 3 Tasks done" in it. The card's link is now its title alone, so it finds the card by its name as an `article` instead; the words stay. The build adds its own tests.

## Group by Initiative: what it proposes

One recommendation for each question, with why.

1. **The switch:** "Group by Initiative", the `packages/ui` `Switch` (size sm, with its label), at the **end of the filter bar's first row**, set apart by a hairline, rather than beside the Initiative select. It changes how the board is laid out, not which Tasks it shows, so it sits apart from the filters, and Clear, which clears filters, leaves it as it is. While it is on and more than one group shows, **Fold all** (Unfold all once every group is folded) sits beside it. At 390px it takes a line of its own under the filters.
2. **The address:** `?group=initiative`, beside the filters' keys, read in the board's route next to `boardFiltersOf` by its own function, any other value left out. A link with it opens the board grouped, and a reload keeps it. Going to Work from the rail opens it ungrouped, as it opens with no filters.
3. **Saved views keep filters only**, not the grouping. A view is "on" while the filters are its own (`sameBoardFilters`); if it held the grouping, turning the switch would turn the view off, and picking a view would switch the grouping under the user. The glossary's Saved view, "a named set of the Work board's filters", stays true. Picking a view keeps the grouping as it is.
4. **A group's header**, on one line where it fits:
   - the **fold**, the `packages/ui` `FoldButton`, holding the **name**, which wraps however long it is;
   - the **state badge** (`InitiativeStateBadge`);
   - its **Projects** (`InitiativeProjects`);
   - **progress**: a small `ProgressBar` and `progressWords`, such as "1 of 6 Tasks done · 2 ready to start", from `initiativeProgress` over every Task of the Initiative, archived and in Shaping too;
   - at the end, the **count of its cards**: "4 on the board", or "3 of 7 shown" while a filter hides some of them (the Project filter included);
   - **Open Initiative**, a link to `/work/initiatives/$initiativeId`. The name folds rather than links, as a fold's line does everywhere else in the app.

   At 390px the name takes the first line; the Projects, the bar and the link's words fold away, leaving the badge, the progress words, the count and the link's arrow.
5. **No Initiative:** a group of its own named **No Initiative**, with "Tasks that aren't part of an Initiative" where an Initiative's badge and progress would be, its count, and no link. It comes **last**, under every Initiative, so the board reads as the Initiatives first, and the work outside them after.
6. **Folding:** a folded group shows its header and, under it, its cards per column, the empty ones left out ("Backlog 1 · In progress 1 · Done 2"); at 390px the header's count alone. A fold is **remembered in this browser**, per group (by the Initiative's id, or `none`), in local storage, as `record-folds.ts` keeps a record's blocks: the board is opened often, and folding the same groups again each time would be a chore. The address doesn't carry it: a shared link shouldn't fold someone's groups, and one key per group would make the address long. A **Done or Dropped Initiative's group starts folded**, as nothing in it waits on anyone, until the user unfolds it.
7. **The order of the groups:** as the Initiatives tab lists them, **newest first** (`GET /initiatives` already answers in that order), with the Active ones before those that ended, and No Initiative last. Initiatives have no manual order, and an order taken from the cards (the board order of each group's first Task) would move whole groups as a card is dragged, its priority changes or it starts, under the user's pointer. An Initiative still being shaped has its Tasks off the board, so it has no group. A Done or Dropped Initiative keeps a group while it has cards on the board, until they are archived.
8. **The filters:**
   - **The Project filter** narrows the cards as today; a group with no card in the Projects shown leaves the board. The header's Projects and progress stay the Initiative's own, as they are its facts; its count says how many of its cards show. The note of how many more Tasks are in other Projects stays above the board, as today.
   - **The Initiative filter** shows that group alone, always open, with no fold (its name shows plain), and Fold all isn't offered. Its options stay as today, so they include an Initiative still being shaped. When the group has no card to show, one line under its header says why, in place of six empty columns: "Its Tasks join the board once the Initiative is confirmed." (being shaped), "None of its Tasks is on the board." (all archived), "Its Tasks on the board are in Projects the Project filter hides.", or "None of its Tasks on the board matches the filters."
   - **Search, Priority and Blocked** narrow each group's cards. A group they leave with no card is left out, and a line under the groups names those left out ("No Task matches the filters in Run environments on Linux, Billing export, so their groups are left out"), so nothing disappears unexplained. When no group is left, one block says nothing matches.
   - **The column counts:** while the columns sit in a row, their heads show once, above the groups, sticking to the top as the page scrolls, each counting the cards shown in all the groups. Once the columns wrap, each group's columns carry their own head with the count in that group.
9. **Drag and drop:** within a column **inside the card's own group** only. While a card is dragged, the one column of its group it can land in is outlined. `placementAmong` is given that group's cards in the column, so the card lands between the neighbours the user dropped it between; dropped among another priority, it takes it, as today. A drop into another group is refused with a toast, "A card moves only within its own group: the board doesn't change a Task's Initiative.", and a drop into another column with today's. A Task's Initiative is set when the Task is added to it, and the board never changes it. The note under the board says "…within its column, inside its own group…".
10. **The board's width** (proposed for the board with grouping off too, as a group's columns follow the same rule): the six columns sit in a row while the page is about 1250px wide or more (columns of about 190px), wrap into two rows of three below that, and stack, one under another, on a page under about 640px. Today the board scrolls sideways under about 1400px, which a group can't do: each group would scroll on its own and its columns would lose the heads above them. Stacked, an empty column takes one line, and inside a group it leaves the stack. Nothing scrolls sideways at any width.

**Jira's group by epic**, the reference: Jira lays a board out in swimlanes, one per epic, under column heads shared by all of them, each swimlane with a header that folds, and the issues without an epic in a swimlane of their own at the bottom. This design takes those: one group per Initiative under the column heads shown once, a header that folds, Fold all, groups with nothing to show left out, and No Initiative last. It doesn't take:

- dragging a card into another swimlane to change its epic: a Task's Initiative is set when it is added to it, so a drop there is refused;
- a "Group by" menu with several choices: one switch, since Initiative is the only grouping asked for; a select can replace it if a second comes;
- an epic's colour on its swimlane: Initiatives have none, and a colour on a card is its Project's mark;
- ordering the swimlanes by the epics' rank: Initiatives have no rank, so newest first, as the Initiatives tab;
- a board that scrolls sideways: the columns wrap and stack instead.

It adds what an epic's swimlane header doesn't carry: the Initiative's state, its Projects, its progress over every Task, and the link to its page.

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: Group by Initiative (the switch), a group and its header, No Initiative (the group), Fold all and Unfold all, "on the board" and "shown" (the header's count), and Grouping off and Grouping on (the scenarios).

### What the build needs that the app doesn't have

- **No service change and no new data.** `Task.initiativeId` puts each card in its group, and `GET /initiatives` gives each header its name, state, Projects and order (newest first). Progress is worked out in the web app by `initiativeProgress` from `GET /tasks`, which already holds every Task, archived and in Shaping too, as the Initiatives tab does it.
- **No contract change.** The grouping stays out of `BoardFilters`, as a Saved view doesn't hold it: the board's route reads `group` beside the filters, and `withoutBoardFilters` already keeps a key that isn't a filter, so Clear and picking a view leave it.
- **The folds' memory:** a small store for the groups' folds in this browser, made as `record-folds.ts`'s is (its own storage key, the same pattern), never sent to the service.
- **The board's columns in one part**, given a set of cards, so a group draws them as the board does. With grouping on, the column heads move above the groups while the columns sit in a row. The wrap at 1250px and 640px is a container query on the page's width, as `work.css` already uses for the Initiatives tab.
- **[Spec 2.1](../spec/2.1-work.md)**, beside "Saved views and the Project filter": "The Work board can be grouped by Initiative: a group for each Initiative with cards on the board, newest first, Active ones before those that ended, then No Initiative; the grouping is kept in the address, never in a Saved view; a group's fold is remembered in this browser. A card is dragged only within its column inside its own group."
- **[Spec 4](../spec/4-ux-direction.md)**'s Work board line, or **[spec 4.1](../spec/4.1-ui-rules.md)**'s Narrow rule: "The Work board's six columns sit in a row while the page has room for them, in two rows of three below that, and stacked on a narrow page; the board doesn't scroll sideways."
- **No test moves:** `apps/web/e2e/work.e2e.ts` drags cards found by their role and runs at 1600px wide, where the six columns still sit in a row. The build adds its own tests.

### About the mock

- Its data follows `Initiative`, `Task` and `QueuedTask` in `packages/contracts/src/work.ts`: five Initiatives across the-office, not-astray and notnullsoft, one with a long name, one Done (Billing export), one still being shaped (with no group, as its Task is in Shaping), two archived Tasks counted in progress but off the board, Tasks with no Initiative, and cards in every column, Queued included. Its search matches titles only, standing in for the service's.
- The Project filter is a select in the top bar, and Open Initiative shows a toast rather than leaving the page.
- Like the chat drawer's page, it uses the Geist fonts only where they are installed.

## Add a Project: what it proposes

1. **Windows pinned to the top:** every window's top sits 7% from the top of the browser window, 24px at least, rather than in the middle. That is where the large window already sits, 86% high and centred, so the large one doesn't move and the small and medium ones line up with it. A window that grows then grows downwards only.
2. **One height for Add a Project:** a medium window that keeps one height, 640px or less in a short browser window, as the Stage history keeps its own. Its body scrolls; its head and its bar stay. Together with 1, neither the top nor the bar moves when the source changes. The cost: a folder or a git URL leaves room under it.
3. **Two steps:** step 1 is Where it is (A folder, A git URL, From GitHub), each ending the same way: a folder's path then Continue, a git URL then Clone, or a pick from GitHub, which clones at once. Step 2 shows the source as one line (what it is, the path or repository, where it was cloned) with Change, then the form's fields as today, regrouped but not changed. Change goes back to step 1 and forgets the clone, as "Pick another repository" does today. The bar's hint says "Step 1 of 2" or "Step 2 of 2".
4. **From GitHub, the list:** a card drawn after `PickList`, with a head (its name and count) and a foot ("Missing one? Install the App on more repositories"). The search box above it stays put and only the rows scroll. Each row shows the repository's name, wrapping however long it is; under it the owner, its default branch and when it was last pushed; then its description on one line, and Private at its end. A repository already added as a Project shows Added and "Already added, as the Project …", and can't be picked. Those rows sit at the end of the list under an "Already added" label, in every design and in search results too, because the repositories pushed most recently are usually the ones already added, and the top rows should be ones the user can pick. The search matches name, owner and description and marks the match.
5. **Three list designs to choose from:**
   1. **Recent first:** one list of all of them, the most recently pushed on top.
   2. **By owner:** an owner filter in the list's head (All and each owner, with counts), recent first within it. With many owners it would be a select.
   3. **Search first:** the five most recently pushed that aren't Projects yet, and the search reaching all of them.
6. **States:** while the list loads, rows shaped like it hold its place (the Loaders proposal's `rows`, fading in after 200ms, so a fast answer shows no flash), and the search box is there at once. No match, and no repository reachable, say so in the card; a failed read shows its problem there with Try again. Signed out, or the App not registered, replaces the search and the list with one block: what to do, Open Settings › Office identity, and Use a git URL instead. Picking a repository shows the clone's progress in its row, the other rows waiting; a failed clone shows its problem in the row with Try again, and a clone refused before it starts keeps its alert above the list.

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: Today, Proposed, pinned to the top, the source line, Recent first, By owner and Search first.

### What the build needs that the app doesn't have

- **`pushedAt` and `description` on `GitHubRepository`**, read from GitHub's repository list (its `pushed_at` and `description`), so the list can sort by when each was last pushed and show a description.
- **Loading the list when the window opens**, rather than when From GitHub is chosen, so it is usually there by then. Which repositories are already Projects comes from the Projects' Repository URLs, already in the app; a Project added from a folder has none, so its repository isn't marked unless the build also reads the folder's remote.
- **The shared loader:** the Loaders design was never built, so the build either builds `BlockLoader` with its `rows` shape, or only the rows this list needs, in its place.
- **`keepsHeight` for a medium window:** `Dialog` allows it only when large today (`packages/ui/src/overlays.tsx`, `dialog.css`). Medium keeps `min(640px, 100%)`.
- **The window rule in [spec 4.1](../spec/4.1-ui-rules.md)'s Windows:** "Every window's top sits 7% from the top of the browser window, at least 24px, so windows line up and grow downwards", and "A window whose content changes as the user moves inside it (the Stage history, Add a Project) keeps one height" in place of the Stage history alone. In `dialog.css`, the backdrop places its window at the top with that padding instead of centring it.
- **Which e2e tests move:** `apps/web/e2e/office-identity.e2e.ts` (the GitHub picker: search, picking, signed out and not registered) and `apps/web/e2e/projects.e2e.ts` (Add a Project from a folder and by a git URL), for step 1's Continue, the source line and Change, and the list's new rows. Any test that finds a window by where it sits on screen moves with the rule.

## Loaders: what it proposes

1. **One loader**, `BlockLoader` in `apps/web/src/shell/`, its bars the design system's `Skeleton`. It is `role="status"` with a visually hidden label, "Loading…" unless the block names what it loads; the bars stay hidden from screen readers. Six shapes: `lines` (text, a form, a set shape such as the ssh key's bar and buttons), `rows` (a list in a card), `cards` (item cards with their frame, as on the Board and Now, whose sunken surface would swallow bare bars), `tiles` (People, Projects), `table` (in place of `DataTable`'s own loading rows) and `turns` (a Thread's messages).
2. **What shows at once:** everything that doesn't wait on data, such as a card's title and note, the filter bar, the Hire a Role and Add a Project tiles. Only the part that waits gets the loader, so the page keeps its frame.
3. **Timing:** it fades in 200ms after it appears, by CSS (160ms), so a quick answer shows no flash. It shows only on the first load (`isLoading`, never `isPending`); a background refresh leaves the content alone. With reduced motion on, the bars hold still: `Skeleton` sets its shimmer inline, so that change goes into `Skeleton` itself.
4. **Empty and failed:** an empty message, or a count of 0, shows only once the data has loaded; while loading, the Board's columns and Now's headings show no count, and the chat's person picker doesn't say "No Threads yet". A failed read shows its problem where the loader was, in every block that reads it, including those that are blank for good today when a read fails: Unconfirmed hosts, Now, the chat drawer's Thread list, and every block waiting on several reads.
5. **The chat drawer** opens on the newest Thread, so it loads in the `turns` shape from the start, and the Thread's messages keep that one loader; on the Thread list it shows `rows`.
6. **Proposed for [spec 4.1](../spec/4.1-ui-rules.md)'s Words**, after "an empty block says what would fill it and how": "A block whose data hasn't arrived yet shows the shared loader, shaped like what will replace it, from 200ms on; its empty message shows only once the data has loaded, and a failed read shows its problem instead. A refresh keeps what is on screen. Small inline bits (a chip, a name, a count) show nothing until they load."

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: the loader (`BlockLoader`) and its shapes.

## The chat drawer: what it proposes

One recommendation for each of the five questions. Stanley (UI/UX Designer) was consulted and worked the Stage; their advice is folded in, and **Moved:** marks where it changed the starting position.

1. **The button** sits at the top bar's right end, after + Idea, right above where the drawer opens, so it is on every page. It has three looks: **nothing new** (its icon and "Chat"), **someone answering** (that person's avatar with a live dot) and **someone has answered** (the avatar of whoever answered, two at most, with an Ember dot). It shows a face and a dot, never a number, so it never reads as the Needs you count in the status strip. Answered wins over answering, as it is the one that waits on the user. Pressing it with an answer waiting opens that Thread; otherwise it opens where the user left off. At 600px and below it shows its mark alone. **Moved:** the answered look leads to the Thread that answered rather than only marking it.
2. **Picking a person:** with no chat open yet, the drawer opens on the people with chat on (Pam, Dwight, Michael, Angela, Jan and Stanley), each with their Role and where their chat stands: Answered, Answering… or when it last moved. Jim and Oscar, with chat off, aren't offered; a line says that people with chat off aren't listed and where chat is turned on. Once a chat is open, the person's name in the head is the switch: a menu of the same people with their dots, which switches without closing the drawer, and links to the person's page. **Moved:** the order is fixed and never sorts by who answered, so a row never moves under the pointer.
3. **Threads in a narrow drawer:** one column. The open Thread fills the drawer, in Rows, under its own row: "‹ Threads", the Thread's title and the Details switch, which serves that Thread only (spec 4.1). The head carries the line on what the person can do. In a Caretaker's Thread, the Allow changes bar sits under the Thread's row, and Allow changes opens its small window. The Thread list is a step back, with New thread at its top; each Thread shows its title, the cache dot, when it last moved and whether it answered. A person with no Threads gets an empty state that says what a Thread is, with New thread. **Moved:** "‹ Threads" carries the Ember dot when another of that person's Threads has answered, so an answer is never hidden one step back.
4. **Beside the page:** the drawer **pushes the page aside** on windows wider than 900px, with no dimmed backdrop: the page gives up the drawer's width and stays whole and usable. Over the page, it would cover the right side, which on a Task or an Initiative page is the conversation and its composer, and on Now the third panel. Dragging its left edge (or ← and → on the focused edge) resizes it between 360px and 720px, leaving the page at least 480px beside it, or 240px in view when it lies over the page, room for the conversation drawer's handle; the default is 420px, a double press on the edge goes back to it, and the width is remembered in this browser, as the rail's toggle is. **Moved:** the drawer sits below the top bar and the status strip, so the button that closes it and the Needs you count stay in reach. **Moved:** from 601px to 900px it lies over the page with no backdrop, as there is no room to push; it fills the window only at 600px and below, since at 900px a full-window chat would hide the page being asked about. Nothing scrolls sideways at any width.
5. **With everything else:**
   - The **Needs you panel** opens over the chat drawer, with its dimmed backdrop, as today: a short visit to answer and close. Beside it, 560px and 420px together would leave little page. The chat stays open under it.
   - A **window** opens over everything, side panels included, such as a Decision's window opened from a Mention in a chat.
   - On a narrow Task or Initiative page, the **conversation drawer's handle** keeps to the page's side of the chat drawer, so neither covers the other's control; the conversation drawer, opened last, lies over the chat.
   - **Esc** closes one thing per press. Drafts are kept throughout, per Thread, as today.

     | Press | What closes |
     | --- | --- |
     | 1 | A window: a Decision's, Allow changes, the Attachment viewer |
     | 2 | A menu or picker inside a panel: the person switch, the mention picker |
     | 3 | The side panel opened last: the chat drawer, the Needs you panel or the conversation drawer |
     | 4 | The next side panel, by the same rule |

     **Moved:** a menu or picker gets its own place in the order, and Esc never steps back inside the drawer, from a Thread to the list.

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: the chat drawer, the Chat button and its looks (nothing new, someone answering, someone has answered or Answered), the person picker, the person switch, the Thread list, over the page and pushes the page aside.

### What the build needs that the app doesn't have

- **A record of what the user has seen in a Thread.** A Thread is only `waiting` or `answering`, and nothing records what the user has seen, so "someone has answered" needs one: the last message seen in each Thread, set when the Thread is on screen in the open drawer. The Task page's unseen counts are kept per page view only today (`threads-seen.ts`) and could move to the same record.
- **The drawer stays open across pages.** The chat lives in the address (`?chat=…&thread=…`) today, which each navigation drops. The drawer's state (open, person, Thread, width) belongs to the shell, and a link that opens a chat, such as a Link card's or the People page's, sets it.
- **The page gives up its width.** To push the page aside, the shell's layout gives the drawer a column below the status strip, and a page folds its side column into its drawer by its own width (844px, the 900px rule less the rail) rather than the window's. That changes spec 4.1's Narrow rule, and the conversation drawer's handle anchors to the page's right edge.
- **Esc by the order things opened.** `useCloseOnEscape` finds the topmost thing by the order of `aria-modal` elements in the document. The chat drawer isn't modal, so "the side panel opened last" needs a shared record of what opened when, in `packages/ui`.
- **A resizable side panel**, a new general part for `packages/ui`, with its edge, its limits and its remembered width.
- **What changes in the spec:** spec 4's "Chats open in a modal", spec 4.1's "A chat's modal is not a window" and its Narrow rule, and the click-through's chat. The chat modal's Thread view, Allow changes bar and Thread items carry over.

## Round 2: what it changes

- **Backlog:** a Task whose Plan and Definition of Done the user wrote and confirmed offers Mark Ready beside Plan with…, so Planning is optional (the user's answer of 2026-11-04; spec 2.1 changes with task 51).
- **Before Ready:** a read-only checklist. Confirm goes back to the Plan's and the Definition of Done's blocks, and the Planner's Pipeline proposal sits inside the rail, at its bottom, centred. With nothing waiting, Before Ready takes the Needs you card's place.
- **The rail:** a loop-back is drawn square, up, across and down, with small rounded corners.
- **A Description** (2026-11-05), optional, above the Plan: what the user wants in their own words, so a Task is more than a placeholder title. In Backlog the Planner's tab offers Make a plan, which plans from the title and the Description with nothing more to write. A new glossary term.
- **The Stage history** lists the runs on the left in a line or two each: number, state, times, tokens and the gist of the outcome. Everything about the run picked shows on the right. An Agent run has two tabs: Summary, with the outcome and its Handover note at reading width, and Thread, which follows a running Stage live and takes a message to it, landing at the next safe point; the Details switch sits on the Thread tab's row, as only the Thread has tool activity. In a Thread a Handover note shows its first lines and "Read it in full", which opens the Summary. A Project command's run shows its whole log. It opens in a large window (the user's choice of 2026-11-05, over a side panel and a view in place of the page), with the Pipeline in small at its top, each Stage's mark and name, to move between them.
- **Windows** (2026-11-05) come in three sizes, small, medium and large, so the whole app reads the same; the Stage history, a Decision's and the Gate's windows are large. Every window has a head with what it is and its X, and a bar at the foot with its actions on the left and Close, always, on the right. An action that needs a step first opens the step over the bar, and the bar's actions become the step's: the Gate's Send back with notes… shows where to and the notes, with Send back and Cancel. In a Decision's window you pick an answer, the recommended one first chosen, or your own words, then Answer.
- **Layout:** Needs you and Before Ready sit above the tabs in the record's column; the conversation keeps its own column, at the same widths.
- **A Decision** with nothing to look at is answered in place, each answer on its own line and the user's own words last. One with Attachments shows its question, the recommended answer with Accept, and Open details, a window with the context, every answer and the pictures. **The Gate** shows what it waits for, Continue and Show details, a window with its four facts, this round's pictures, Open Changes, Send back with notes… and Drop.
- **The conversation** has a tab for the Planner and for each person who ran a Stage of the Task, each with that person's one Thread on the Task, written in at any time (task 49, pulled into task 51). The composer always writes in that tab's Thread; its To line marks the cache warm, with the minutes left of its hour, or cold, before the person's name, and it offers Auto, Continue and Start fresh with what each does. While the person's Stage runs, the tab says so and links to the Stage history, the only place to write into that work. Stage Threads live only in the Stage history.
- **Components:** where a part of today's page looks almost like a `packages/ui` component (a button, a select, a tab) but is styled on its own, the build uses the component. Parts made for one feature, such as the rail, stay.

## The chat flow: Chapters

The user chose Chapters from the three flows compared here. Each Thread is a box. The Thread you're on and any Thread with an open Decision stay unfolded; the others fold to one line with their Handover note, so a long Task stays short. One author's neighbouring messages join into a turn, regular messages sit on the plain surface, and your own stay neutral. Decisions, Handover notes and a failed run's line keep their colour. "Chapter" is this design's name, not a glossary term: in the app it is simply a Thread.

## What the implementation must keep

- **Attachments by round:** the Attachments tab uses the app's `AttachmentGallery` and `attachmentRounds`: the newest round open on top, earlier rounds folded under "Round n" with the send-back's feedback, and a viewer that steps through every picture. Moving the Attachments to a tab must not lose this.
- **One screenshot size:** 160 by 100 wherever a screenshot shows, as `.attachment-thumbnail` in the app. In Needs you, the screenshots scroll past a height of 176px, so the card stays small however many a Task has.

## What a proposal changes

[Spec 4](../spec/4-ux-direction.md) and the click-through are the UI spec. They change only when an implementation Task adopts a design from here and the user agrees to the change; that Task then updates spec 4 and the click-through with it, as task 48 did for the Task page. Where the mock-up and the task differ, the task's [What was settled](../tasks/48-task-page-redesign.md#what-was-settled) stands.

## Opening a page

Each page is one self-contained file, with its CSS, script and fonts inline: open it in a browser by double-click, offline. It makes no network requests.

Its colours, type and density are copied from The Office's tokens (`design-system/tokens/`, `packages/ui/src/appearance/palettes/{light,dark}.css` and `packages/ui/src/appearance/densities/comfortable.css`), so when those change, a page here keeps the values it was made with. Its tabs, buttons, badges, avatars, cards and messages are re-drawn after `packages/ui` and `apps/web/src/shell/conversation/`, so an implementation Task uses those components rather than copying this CSS. The app's themed scrollbars are in `packages/ui`. Its mock data follows the shapes of `Task`, `TaskStage`, `Thread` and `Decision` in `packages/contracts`, with a few fields for display only (people's names, messages, which Definition of Done statements are met).
