## The Decision form card: what it proposes

From [spec 2.4](../spec/2.4-conversations.md)'s Decision, [spec 4](../spec/4-ux-direction.md)'s Decision bullets and the glossary's Decision, Question, Choice, Open question, Comment, General comment, Submit, Ask me again and Asked again, as the Task "Spec and glossary: the Decision form" published them. Today's card (`decision-card.tsx`) answers one question with one click per option and a free answer; the form keeps its look (the amber card, the Decision badge, who asks, the recommendation's words) and changes what is inside. One recommendation for each open point, with why.

1. **A choice is `RadioList`, with "None of these: answer in words" as its last line.** The form is sent once, by Submit, so an option is picked, not sent: one click per option, as today, would send the form at the first pick. `RadioList` is the design system's form of "pick one", already used where answering is a form (spec 4.1), and its `aside` holds the **Recommended** badge. The spec lets a choice be answered by its comment alone, with no option picked; radios can't be unpicked, so the last line says it in words, and picking it turns the question's comment into **"Your answer in words"**, which then must be written. Picking an option again keeps those words as the comment. Under the options, "Jim recommends “IndexedDB”: drafts can hold pictures…" says the reason, as today's card does.
2. **Each question takes its comment behind "Add a comment"**, a `TextButton`, so a form of several questions doesn't open with a box under each; a comment once written stays open. An open question is a `Textarea`, "Your answer", required. The **general comment** is always shown under the questions, labelled Optional: it is the one place to say something about the form as a whole, and Ask me again sends it.
3. **Submit, then Ask me again, then the progress line.** Submit is the primary action and, in place, it is the one click the spec asks for: every recommended option is already picked, so a form of choices goes "as recommended" in one press. A separate Accept in place would be a second button doing the same, so **Accept is the compact card's** (below). Submit stays disabled until every question is answered and says which on hover: "Answer question 3 first", "Answer question 2 in its words first", or with one question "Pick an option or write your answer first" (spec 4.1's disabled control says why). With several questions, the **progress line** at the actions' right end reads "2 of 3 answered" with a small meter (spec 4.1's figure against a limit) and "Question 3 needs an answer", a link that jumps there; untouched recommendations count as answered. Each question's number turns green once answered and is dashed amber while it needs an answer, so the form shows at a glance what is left.
4. **Ask me again opens a step**, inside the card or over the window's bar (spec 4.1's step before an action): "What should Jim explain or change?", the general comment, Optional, with **Ask me again** and Cancel. It says that only this comment is sent and that the picks stay as a draft until something is sent. **The Office's own Decisions show no Ask me again** at all, rather than a disabled one: nobody is there to ask again, so there is nothing to explain on hover.
5. **The compact card, past a threshold: more than 3 questions, or any Attachment** (open for the Gate). Spec 4 asks for it when a form has Attachments "or more questions than fit in the card". Three questions in place are about a screen's height in Needs you at 900px; a fourth pushes Submit out of view, and pictures need the window's room. The compact card shows the context's first line, **question 1** with its recommended option and reason, "7 more questions, 3 of them open questions", the progress line, **Open details**, and **Accept** while every question is a choice (spec 4: Submit with every recommended option and no comments). Once the draft differs from the recommendations, Accept gives way to a "Draft kept · 2 changes" badge and Open details becomes the main action, so one click never sends something other than what the user picked. Weighed: a threshold by height (measured) would make the same Decision compact in the panel and whole on the Task page; a count is the same everywhere.
6. **Open details: list and details** (spec 4.1), open for the Gate against one scrolling form. The Large window keeps one height. Left, the questions, one line or two each, with each one's answer so far ("IndexedDB (recommended)", "In words: …", "Needs an answer" or "Needs an answer in words" in amber), the progress line above them; right, the picked question as in place, with the context (and the pictures, 160 by 100, and file chips) folded above it, and Previous and Next. The **bar** holds the general comment on one line, then Submit and Ask me again, and Close on the right. Under 900px the list becomes a **row of numbers** above the question, each green, amber or outlined as it is answered, needs an answer, or is open, as a list of eight lines would push the question below the fold. Why list and details: every question can be reached in one press and the user sees what is left without scrolling, which a grilling form of 8 or more needs; the progress line's link picks the missing question. **One scrolling form** (the switcher's "Long form's window") is the alternative: the same form as in place, numbered, with the progress line pinned at its top. It reads more like the card and suits 4 or 5 questions; it loses sight of what is left on a long form. A one-question window (one with Attachments) is always the scrolling form.
7. **Drafts follow the Decision, and closing the window keeps them** (open for the Gate). The picks and words are kept per Decision in the browser tab while the user moves between Needs you, the Needs you panel, Now, the Thread and the window: the same draft shows wherever the card does. So the window doesn't ask "Close without saving?" (spec 4.1's Closing lets a window say otherwise): nothing is lost by closing it, and the card it came from shows "Draft kept". Submit and Ask me again end the draft; a refused send keeps it. Weighed: asking on close would treat the window as the only place the answers live, when the same form is on the card underneath.
8. **Where it shows** (spec 2.4, spec 4):
   - **A Task's Needs you:** the card, answered there, whole or compact.
   - **The Task's Thread:** in short while it waits, "8 questions, starting with: …", "Waits on you. Answer it in Needs you" (or, put to a Consultant, "Take it over in Needs you · Open the Consultation"); whole once answered, asked again or closed.
   - **Now and the Needs you panel:** each entry is **one line**, "Decision · 8 questions · Jim · asked 10:42", then its Task's title and Project chip (or the Initiative's title and "On no Task"), then the same card without its head, answered as in Needs you.
   - **An Initiative's shaping or planning Thread:** a Decision on no Task is the card itself in the Thread, answered there, as the Initiative page has no Needs you card, with "On no Task: about the Initiative as a whole"; it is in the Needs you panel under the Initiative's title. A planning Thread's Decision on one of its Tasks names it with the Task's chip and shows on that Task's page as well. In a planning Thread, while Angela is answering, Submit and Ask me again stay enabled and say "Reaches Angela when this turn ends.", and the sent card keeps that line until the turn ends; in a shaping Thread they are disabled as elsewhere (spec 2.4: only a planning Thread records an answer while the Planner works).
   - **Inline in a chat**, in the chat drawer: the card in the Thread, answered and sent there; a long one's Open details opens the window over the drawer. Not in Needs you, not counted in the status strip, no Notification.
   - **On a Task only:** The Office's own Decision (it comes from a Stage), closed by a dropped Task, put to a Consultant (the user takes over a call only on a Task) and the approval. The switcher greys these out at the Initiative's Thread and the chat drawer, saying why.
9. **Once sent, read-only where it was asked, without the amber.** Each question with its number, the question in small, its answer ("(recommended)" when it was the recommended option; a choice answered in words shows the words as the answer), its comment, then the general comment, and an outcome line: "Answered by you · 11:06", another user's name, "Answered by Ana" (spec 2.4: any user answers any Decision), "Answered by Michael in a Consultation · Open the Consultation", "Answered by Pam at your request" with the link to the Thread the user asked in. Past 3 questions it shows the first 3 and **Show all 8**. Asked again: the questions, "Not answered", and "Asked again by you: <general comment>"; the new form follows in the Thread. Closed: "Closed: the Task was dropped". The amber goes once nothing waits on the card, so in a long Thread what still waits stands out; the Decision badge keeps its words.
10. **The states around it**, each in words already in the app where they exist: put to a Consultant, on a Task, "Waiting on Michael in a Consultation. Answer here to take it over."; while the asker answers, Submit, Accept and Ask me again disabled with "Jim is still answering. Answer once they finish."; refused, an `Alert` "The answer wasn't sent" with the reason, under the general comment, the draft kept; and "A question first? Ask Jim in the Thread; the Decision stays open." under the actions.
11. **The approval stays a card of its own**, red-edged with its shield, beside the form in Needs you and the Needs you panel: "Jim asks to: Install ffmpeg on the machine", the command whole, why, the Hard switch's name and words, and **Allow once** and **Deny**. No comment, no Ask me again, never a question of a form (spec 4, the glossary). ## Live view: what it proposes

For the Initiative "Office visualisation", before its three build Tasks start. The prototype holds a short version under its frame; this section holds the reasons. One recommendation for each question, with why.

1. **The art: drawn in code for The Office, under GPL-3.0-only.** No openly licensed pack fits, so the fallback the Task allows is the recommendation. The packs weighed:

   | Pack | Licence | Why not |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Kenney, Furniture Kit | CC0 | Rendered 3D furniture, not pixel art; no office computers, no characters to match it. |
   | Liberated Pixel Cup (LPC) character generator | CC-BY-SA 3.0, GPL 3.0 or OGA-BY 3.0, per asset | Layered and recolourable, but drawn for a top-down ¾ view, not isometric, and every asset needs its own credit. |
   | CC0 isometric characters on OpenGameArt | CC0 | No separate layers for body, hair and clothes, so no recolouring by palette and no generated variety. |
   | Paid isometric office packs | Commercial | Not openly licensed. |

   **Chosen:** shapes and figures drawn by the page's own script. Source: [`docs/design/live-view.html`](live-view.html); licence: GPL-3.0-only, The Office's own; author: The Office. It fits the 2:1 tiles exactly, is layered by construction, recolours by palette, and needs no download and no credit per asset. The build moves the grids into `apps/web/src/surfaces/live-view/art/` with a `CREDITS.md` beside them saying so.
2. **Rendering:** 2:1 isometric tiles of 32 × 16 art pixels. Each sprite is a grid of palette indexes (a desk with one to three monitors, a chair's seat and its back, a sofa, a plant, a figure in a pose), painted once per palette into a canvas: in PixiJS, one `Texture`. The world is drawn back to front by depth (x + y of what stands on the floor, a seated person just in front of their desk, a chair's back in front of them) into one buffer at one art pixel per pixel, then drawn scaled by a whole number of device pixels with nearest-neighbour, so pixels stay crisp. The hour's light multiplies each texture once per tint (a sprite's `tint`); lit screens, lamps, the windows' sky and the exit sign are drawn untinted, with soft glows added on top once it is dark.
3. **Generating figures:** a seeded random (FNV-1a of the Role key into mulberry32) draws, always in the same order, a build (slim, regular, broad), a height, a skin tone (6), a hair style (bald, buzz, short, swept, bob, long, bun, curly) and colour (7), a top (tee, hoodie, jacket), an accessory (none, glasses, headphones, a beard, a scarf), trousers (4) and shoes (4): some 290,000 figures. **The clothes are the person's Avatar tint**, worked out from their name as `Avatar` does: the top in the tone's `-700`, its trim in `-100`. Why: the same key gives the same figure everywhere with nothing stored, and the figure matches the Avatar on its nameplate. A renamed person keeps their figure and may change tint, as their Avatar does. Each figure is layered (body, clothes, hair, accessory), so recolouring is a palette swap; its poses are standing, walking (four frames, each way), sitting at work (typing), hand raised, asleep, turned round to talk, standing to talk, holding a mug or a paper cup and sipping from it, sitting on a floor pillow with a game controller, and panicking at the fire alarm. Anyone standing or sitting still now and then **glances** aside: the head turns a little to one side for 1 to 2 seconds, every 6 to 20, at times picked by the Role key and the time.

## Initiative blocks in the columns: what it proposes

From the user's note on Work › Tasks with Group by Initiative off ("I want tasks in each column to be grouped by initiative, like be wrapped in a block with initiative's name"). Today each column is one flat list in the board's order (`BoardColumns` in `board.tsx`, `cardsIn` in `board-columns.ts`), and nothing on a card says its Initiative. One recommendation for each question, with why.

1. **The order: Gathered.** All of an Initiative's cards in a column make **one block, placed where its first card falls** in the column's order; inside it, the column's order (priority, then manual order). A loose card keeps its own place between the blocks.
   - **Why:** one block per Initiative per column is what "wrapped in a block" asks for: each Initiative reads once in a column, and the top of a column still holds its first card by priority. The cost: a card further down joins its block higher up, so between blocks the order holds by their first cards only, and a block moves when its first card changes (dragged to the end of its block, or given another priority).
   - **Adjacent runs**, the alternative on the page, wraps only cards that sit next to each other: the order stays exact, but one Initiative can show as two or three blocks in a column (Backlog, Planning and Queued in the mock), and blocks split and merge as cards move, which reads as noise and loses "one Initiative, one block".
   - **A fixed order of blocks** (newest Initiative first, as the grouped view) was weighed too: steady under a drag and level across columns, but a High priority card with no Initiative would sink under every block, and lining Initiatives up across columns is what Group by Initiative is for.
   - **Queued** gathers by the same rule over the queue's order: a block sits at its first card's place, its cards by place. As the adopted card shows no place (it is in the clock's popover), the block's head in Queued shows its cards' **places, "#1 · #3"**, where the other columns show a count, so the queue's order still reads across blocks; its tooltip says "Places 1 and 3 in the queue". Queued cards still don't drag.
   - **Done** gathers over its own order: a block sits where its card that ended last falls, its cards the one that ended last on top (`lastEndedFirst`).
   - **Why one rule for every column:** each column applies it to its own order, so nothing new is learned per column; Queued alone needs the places said, because gathering moves a card away from its neighbours in the queue and the card can't tell it.
2. **Tasks with no Initiative: loose cards**, outside any block, each at its own place in the column's order. No "No Initiative" block. **Why:** a block says "these belong together", and Tasks with no Initiative don't. On a board with few Initiatives a No Initiative block would wrap most cards in every column, a frame that says nothing. The grouped view needs its No Initiative group because there every card must sit in a row; here the column already holds them.
3. **A block of one card is still a block. Why:** the frame is how a card says it belongs to an Initiative; a lone card without one would read as a Task with no Initiative. **With the Initiative filter set, no blocks:** the columns are flat, as today. **Why:** every card is then that Initiative's and the filter's select names it; six frames with the same name, one per column, would add a level and say nothing. The Project filter, search, Priority and Blocked narrow a block's cards and its count; a block with no card shown in a column leaves it.
4. **Drag and drop: within its column, inside its own block.** A card in a block drags only inside it; a loose card among the loose cards, landing before the next loose card under the pointer, between blocks too. While a card is dragged, its block is outlined (in the accent, as the grouped view's column) and the rest of its column steps back; for a loose card, the blocks step back.
   - **Dropped among another priority inside its block, it takes that priority**, as today, with today's toast ("… is now high priority."). If it was the block's first card, the block then moves to where its new first card falls (the mock's Drag and drop scenario shows it).
   - **A drop over another Initiative's block is refused**, with the grouped view's toast in this design's word: "A card moves only within its own block: the board doesn't change a Task's Initiative." A loose card dropped on a block: "A card with no Initiative moves only among the cards outside the blocks: the board doesn't change a Task's Initiative." A drop in another column keeps today's toast.
   - **Why not placed and kept in its own block:** the card would take its place among another Initiative's cards in the order and then show back in its own block, so the drop seems to do nothing, or moves its block, and can change its priority unseen (the page's Drop elsewhere › Placed shows it). A Task's Initiative is set when it is added to one, and the board never changes it.
   - The board note under the columns: "Drag a card that has not started to reorder it within its column, inside its Initiative's block; dropping it among another priority takes that priority. Queued cards keep their place in the queue."
5. **No folding, and always on with the switch off**: no switch of its own. **Why:** a block is a part of one column; a fold per block would be up to six folds per Initiative, each hiding two or three cards. Folding a whole Initiative is the grouped view's, and showing one Initiative alone the Initiative filter's. A second switch beside Group by Initiative would be two near-identical layouts to tell apart, and the user asked for the board to read this way.
6. **Telling blocks apart: no colour.** The head's name, the block's frame and its fill (a step darker than the column, a step below the cards) tell a block from the next and from loose cards. To follow one Initiative across columns, **hovering or focusing a block's head outlines its Initiative's blocks in every column**. **Why:** Initiatives have no colour (it would be new data, or a hue picked from the id that means nothing), and the palettes' hues are taken: teal and violet are Projects' marks, amber, blue, green and red are states. A card already carries two colours, its state's edge and its Project's mark; a third on its block reads as one more of those (the page's Block mark › Hue shows the clash).
7. **The block's head**, on one line where it fits: the **flag** (the app's icon for an Initiative, as in mentions and on the Task page) and the **name**, a link to `/work/initiatives/$initiativeId`, wrapping however long it is; the **Paused** badge (`InitiativePausedBadge`, small) while the Initiative is paused; at the end, **how many of its cards the column shows** (in Queued, their places), with its words in a `Tooltip` ("2 of its Tasks in Ready"). No state badge, Projects or progress: those are the grouped view's header, read once per Initiative; repeated in every column they would outweigh the cards.

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: an Initiative block (a block) and its head, loose cards, Gathered and Adjacent runs (the orders), Block mark, Drop elsewhere, Refused and Placed, and the scenarios. Initiative, Task, Queued, Done, Paused, Project filter and Initiative filter are used as the glossary and the app have them.

### Two kinds of hidden information

- **Plain information** (the Usage estimate, what holds a Paused Task, a state, Planning, the Project, the priority, who is working): only the mark shows; its words show in the `Tooltip` on hover and on keyboard focus. ### The popover

- **Opens** on a press of its mark, or Enter or Space on it, under the mark with its left edge on the mark's, above it when there is no room below, kept inside the browser window. Opened by keyboard, focus moves to its first link; Tab walks its links, and Tab past the last closes it and goes on past the mark; Shift+Tab before the first goes back to the mark.
- **Closes** on a press anywhere else (that press then does what it does, so pressing another mark opens that one), on Esc (focus goes back to its mark), on a second press of its mark, and on following one of its links. It follows its mark when the page scrolls and closes once the mark leaves the view.
- **One at a time:** opening one closes any other.
- **No drag, no Task opened:** pressing a mark turns its card's dragging off until the press ends, so a drag never starts from a mark; the mark sits above the title's stretched link, so the press doesn't open the Task; and the popover lies on the page's body, outside the card, so pressing or selecting inside it touches neither.
- **By touch,** where there is no hover, a press on any plain mark shows its words in the same popover, and a press on the rest of the card opens the Task. The mock's Input switch shows it with a mouse; on a touch screen the mock starts in Touch.
- **Not a window** in [spec 4.1](../spec/4.1-ui-rules.md)'s sense, which keeps that word for the `Dialog`: no head, bar or backdrop, and nothing to answer. Esc closes it before any window or side panel, as it does a menu. This is why the user's "tooltip-like window" is called a popover here.

## Sub-agents and processes: what it proposes

The design behind [spec 2.9](../spec/2.9-sub-agents-and-processes.md), which holds the research on what each engine reports today and the model in full. One recommendation for each question, with why.

1. **The names: Sub-agent and Process**, the user's own words, as glossary terms. "Process" was in Pipeline's _Avoid_ list as a synonym for a Pipeline; the entry now says a Process is a session's background command. "Background command" (the API engine's `BackgroundCommands`, Claude Code's own notice words) was weighed and left: longer on a 390px line, and not what the user says. In code a Process is never a variable named `process`, Node's global.
2. **Who keeps them: Conversations**, beside the Thread whose session runs them. The Engine reports them as session events and keeps nothing, as it talks to no module; Pipelines needs nothing, as a Stage's session is a Stage Thread. Conversations already reads every session's events (`ThreadRunner`, `StageThreads`), applies the Project's mask before anything is stored or sent (`sessionLogLines`), and sends a Thread's web events, and a Process's output can hold a secret as a message can. Reviewed with the CTO, who agreed and asked for the changes folded into points 2 to 9.
   - **One id for both kinds:** `callId`, the id of the tool call that started it, which every engine has, a foreground Sub-agent included; each also keeps the engine's task id, `engineTaskId`, when there is one, which stopping it later will need.
   - **Masking whole lines:** a secret split across two output chunks would pass the Project's mask, so Conversations masks a Process's output by complete lines, holding back an unfinished last line until its newline, the Process's end or about a second. The 256 KB cap counts the output after masking.
3. **Four states:** Running, then Finished, Failed or **Stopped**. Stopped is added to the three asked for: the Agent stopping its own dev server with TaskStop is ordinary work, and Failed would cry wolf. The SDK makes the same split. A Stopped one says how: by the Agent, by an interrupt, with the session, or "Ended when The Office restarted": after a restart nothing can see the session, and a background command may still run on the machine, so the ending doesn't claim it stopped.
4. **How long: the rest of the Thread's session**, in the database: across turns, across the idle end and resume (the same session), across a restart (running ones then get the restart's ending), until the Context policy starts a fresh session in that Thread. A Stage run has one session, so its Sub-agents and Processes stay readable in the Stage history with the run.
5. **Caps:** a Process keeps the last 256 KB of output and says how much earlier output isn't kept; a Sub-agent its last 1,000 messages and tool calls; a session its latest 100 ended ones. Output is batched in **one place only**, the engine adapter: each half-second read of the output file is one `process-output` event, which Conversations stores and sends as it comes.
6. **On Claude Code, a Sub-agent starts with its `Task` (or `Agent`) tool call**, which reaches the session for sure on both adapters; the `task_*` messages add its `engineTaskId` and whether it runs in the background (a sixth event, `sub-agent-changed`), and its end. Whether `task_started` comes for a foreground Sub-agent is left to the engine check. **A Process's output is read from its output file**, every half second while it runs, from where the last read stopped, and once more at its end. The stream never carries output; `get_task_output` gives only the last 8 KiB and only the `-p` fallback can send it. The file's place comes from the `Bash` call's result and the end's `task_notification`; the engine check confirms both.
7. **Sub-agents on the API engine: out of scope.** Its loop has no `Task` tool, so it has none to report; the line there counts Processes only. A `Task` tool in The Office's own loop is later work, its own Task.
8. **Live: web events that carry what changed**, not a signal to refetch: `thread.sub-agent-started`/`-changed`/`-ended` and `thread.process-started`/`-ended` carry the whole item, and `thread.sub-agent-messages` and `thread.process-output` the new part with its sequence numbers. **Output reaches only its own Thread:** `EventStream` subscribes by event type alone today (`platform/websocket/event-stream.ts`), so a subscription gains a scope, an event type plus a `threadId`, and the two streams go only to subscriptions scoped to their Thread. A page subscribes to them, scoped, as the view opens and unsubscribes as it closes.
9. **A page opened late:** the list needs no catch-up of its own: each start, change and end carries the whole item, the page puts it in its list by `callId`, and a state never goes back from ended to Running, so these events apply in any order. A Sub-agent's messages and a Process's output catch up by sequence: the page subscribes first and holds what arrives, reads the item up to a sequence, then applies only what comes after it; an event at or before it is dropped, and a gap or a reconnect reads again from the last sequence (`?after=`). The sketch's reference, under the frame, walks through it with chunk numbers.
10. **The line** sits right above the message input, as the Open page line does (`composer-note`), so it is in every place a Thread takes a message: the chat drawer, the Task page's conversation, a Stage run's Thread tab. Where a Thread takes no message, it sits above the line saying why. It shows once the session has had a Sub-agent or a Process: "● 2 sub-agents, 1 process running · 3 ended, 1 failed" (the dot live, "1 failed" in red), "1 sub-agent running", "1 process running"; once none runs, "3 sub-agents, 1 process ended", without the dot. The whole line is one button ending in Show and a chevron (the chevron alone at 390px). It is neutral, not the blue of a note: it tells and asks nothing.
11. **The view** is a list and details (spec 4.1). From the chat drawer and the Task page it opens in a **large window**, "Sub-agents and processes", which keeps one height; its bar says "Read-only: only the Agent can stop them" and has Close. In the **Stage history**, already a window, it is the run's **third tab**, "Sub-agents and processes" with its count, beside Summary and Thread, as no window opens over another; the line in the Thread tab switches to it, and the tab is there only for a run that had any. The view lays itself out by its own width: under 640px the list and the details stack, with "‹ All sub-agents and processes" back to the list.
12. **The list:** Running on top, then Ended, the one that ended last first, each with its count. Each item in a line or two: a mark for its kind, its description (a Process without one shows its command, wrapping), then its kind, its state and how long it ran ("Process · Failed · exit code 1 · 3m 10s", "Sub-agent · Explore · Running · 1m 12s").
13. **A Sub-agent's details:** its description, state, kind and In the background; who started it, when, how long, how many tool calls; **Its prompt**, three lines with Read it in full; **Messages and tool calls** in Rows with its own Details switch on that row, as a Thread has them (its text as turns under "Explore sub-agent", its tool calls folded under them); while it runs, the typing dots; once ended, what it **reported back** to whoever started it, or how it failed.
14. **A Process's details:** its description, state, shell and id; its **Command** whole, wrapping; its **Output** in monospace, following the end while it runs ("Following the end") until the user scrolls up, then **Jump to the end**; only the output scrolls, in its place. Once ended, a strip says how: "Finished with exit code 0", "Failed with exit code 1" in red, "Stopped by the Agent (TaskStop)". The exit code shows only where the engine gives one: the API engine does, and Claude Code's end notice has none (spec 2.9), so there it reads "Failed".
15. **Read-only:** no Stop on an item, no Stop all. Stopping from the view is later work: the SDK's `stopTask`, the fallback's `stop_task` and `BackgroundCommands.stop` exist, but what the Agent is told when the user stops its work is a decision of its own.

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: the line (above the message input), the view and its list and details, Running and Ended (the list's groups), Its prompt, Messages and tool calls, Reported back, Following the end and Jump to the end, and the scenarios. **Sub-agent** and **Process** are glossary terms; Running, Finished, Failed and Stopped are their states.

## The Initiatives tab: what it proposes

Round 2, after the user's note of 2026-11-09 on round 1 (sections by state on one page): just adding grouping isn't enough; the tab should be closer to the Tasks tab's design, and it must hold hundreds or thousands of Initiatives after months or years. So it borrows the two things that keep the Tasks tab readable however much work passes through it: a **board** by state, and the **Archive**. One recommendation for each question, with why.

1. **Grouping by state: a board, as the Tasks tab has.** The tab is the Work board's parts re-used: its filter bar, its columns (a sunken column with a head, a count and a hint) and its cards (one link, a coloured edge, a foot of small facts). Three columns, left to right as an Initiative moves: **Shaping** ("With Michael, until you confirm them"), **Active** ("Their Tasks are on the Work board") and **Done** ("Done and dropped in the last 30 days, then archived"). **Dropped** sits folded at the foot of the Done column, as the Work board's Done column holds Done and Dropped, but never mixed with them: no two states share a list, and the Dropped ones are two columns and a fold away from the Active ones. At 390px the columns stack in the same order.
   A board rather than sections on one page (round 1) or a **List** with a State filter kept in the address (`?state=`, the alternative in the mock): the board is what the user already reads on the Tasks tab, shows every live state at once, and stays one screen long because the Archive takes what ended. The List shows one state at a time, denser, but hides Shaping behind a chip while the user looks at Active, and looks like nothing else on Work but the Archived tab.
2. **Keeping it short for years: the Archive, extended to Initiatives.** A Done or Dropped Initiative leaves the board **once it has ended for the Archive period** (Settings › Office limits, 30 days, the one Tasks use), or at once by **Archive** in its card's More menu. Archived isn't a state: it stays Done or Dropped, its page and Threads stay readable, and **Restore** puts it back in Done for a full period, as for a Task. Archived Initiatives show in the **Archived** tab, beside the archived Tasks, with a `SegmentedControl` between Tasks and Initiatives (`?kind=initiatives`): a search of names and outcomes, a Done / Dropped / All filter with counts, the one that ended last on top under a line per month, **25 to a page** with `Pagination`, and the address keeping the search, the filter and the page. After two years (the mock's "Two years in": about 1,260 Initiatives) the board still holds only what moves and what just ended, about 30 cards, and the other 1,230 are a search away.
3. **The order inside each group:**
   - **Shaping** and **Active:** newest first, by `createdAt`, as `GET /initiatives` already answers. Initiatives have no priority or manual order, and an order by what moves would shift cards under the pointer as Tasks change state; the edges and the Waiting on you chip find what needs the user instead. A manual order, dragged as Tasks are, can come later if the user asks; it isn't proposed.
   - **Done**, its **Dropped** fold and the **archive:** the one that ended last on top. That needs **one new field**, `endedAt`, the time it became Done or Dropped: the Archive period counts from it too, so the build needs it anyway.
4. **Each card:**
   - **Active:** the **name** (the card's one link, wrapping however long it is); its **Projects**; its **outcome**, two lines at most, or "Outcome not written yet."; **progress**, a small `ProgressBar` and `progressWords` ("2 of 7 Tasks done · 1 ready to start"); and a foot with **what moves now**, each only when there is one: "2 waiting on you" (a warning `Badge`), "1 in progress" (In progress or Paused, with the live dot), "1 blocked" (not started, waiting for a Task that isn't Done or Dropped), and at its end the avatars of who is working on its Tasks now, as a Task card's working mark. Its **edge** follows the Task card's colours: amber while a Task waits on the user, green while one is in progress, none otherwise. An Active Initiative with nothing to count says so instead of "0 of 0 Tasks done": "No Task left: every one was dropped. Add a Task or drop the Initiative on its page.", or "No Task yet. Add one on its page."
   - **Shaping:** the name, its Projects (or "No Project yet"), its outcome, and a foot "Shaping with Michael · 2 Tasks so far" (or "No Task yet"), as a Planning card says "Planning with Angela". No bar: none of its Tasks can be done before Confirm.
   - **Done or Dropped:** a compact card: the name; a foot with its Projects, its final count ("3 of 3 Tasks done", "No Task done") and when it ended ("Done 3 Nov", "Dropped 20 Oct"); a green edge for Done, red for Dropped; and a **More** menu (Open, Archive), as `TaskMenu` on a Task card.

The archive's table becomes a list of rows, the name on a line of its own and the state, date and Restore under it. Nothing scrolls sideways.
5. **Finding one:** the filter bar has a **search** of names and outcomes and a **Waiting on you** chip (Initiatives with a Task waiting on the user, with its count), and Clear while either is set; both are kept in the address (`?query=`, `?waiting=1`), as the Work board's filters are. While the search matches archived Initiatives, a line under the bar says so ("2 archived Initiatives match “sync” too.") with **Search the archive**, which opens the archive on the same words. No Saved views: a Saved view is the Work board's (glossary), and two filters don't need one.
6. **Folding:** only **Dropped** folds, at the foot of Done, folded at first; folded, its line shows its count and the latest dropped ("Latest: Slack notifications, 30 Oct"). Its fold is **remembered in this browser**, in local storage as `board-group-folds.ts` keeps the Work board's groups, never in the address: someone looking back over what was dropped goes in and out of Initiative pages and shouldn't unfold it again each time. Shaping, Active and Done don't fold: the Archive keeps them short.
7. **Counts and empty states:**
   - Each column's head counts the cards it shows, the filters and the Project filter applied; the Dropped fold counts its own; the Waiting on you chip counts its matches; the archive's filter chips count theirs, and `Pagination` says "1–25 of 1,230".
   - **Every column always shows**, so the board keeps its shape; with no card, its dashed empty line says what would fill it ("Nothing being shaped. Shape an Idea into an Initiative from Ideas.", "Nothing Active. **This design's names**, not glossary terms: Today, Proposed: Board and Alternative: List (the layouts compared); the Initiatives board, a column and its hint, a card's edge and what moves now; the Dropped fold and its Latest; Open the archive, Search the archive and the archive's month lines; and the scenarios Every state, Dropped unfolded, Search: sync, Waiting on you, Nothing Shaping, The archive, Two years in and No Initiatives. Step 2 shows the source as one line (what it is, the path or repository, where it was cloned) with Change, then the form's fields as today, regrouped but not changed. 4. **From GitHub, the list:** a card drawn after `PickList`, with a head (its name and count) and a foot ("Missing one? Install the App on more repositories"). The search box above it stays put and only the rows scroll. Each row shows the repository's name, wrapping however long it is; under it the owner, its default branch and when it was last pushed; then its description on one line, and Private at its end. A repository already added as a Project shows Added and "Already added, as the Project …", and can't be picked. Those rows sit at the end of the list under an "Already added" label, in every design and in search results too, because the repositories pushed most recently are usually the ones already added, and the top rows should be ones the user can pick. The search matches name, owner and description and marks the match.
5. **Three list designs to choose from:**
   1. **Recent first:** one list of all of them, the most recently pushed on top.
   2. **By owner:** an owner filter in the list's head (All and each owner, with counts), recent first within it. With many owners it would be a select.
   3. **Search first:** the five most recently pushed that aren't Projects yet, and the search reaching all of them.
6. **States:** while the list loads, rows shaped like it hold its place (the Loaders proposal's `rows`, fading in after 200ms, so a fast answer shows no flash), and the search box is there at once. No match, and no repository reachable, say so in the card; a failed read shows its problem there with Try again. Signed out, or the App not registered, replaces the search and the list with one block: what to do, Open Settings › Office identity, and Use a git URL instead. Picking a repository shows the clone's progress in its row, the other rows waiting; a failed clone shows its problem in the row with Try again, and a clone refused before it starts keeps its alert above the list.

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: Today, Proposed, pinned to the top, the source line, Recent first, By owner and Search first.

Which repositories are already Projects comes from the Projects' Repository URLs, already in the app; a Project added from a folder has none, so its repository isn't marked unless the build also reads the folder's remote.

## Loaders: what it proposes

1. **One loader**, `BlockLoader` in `apps/web/src/shell/`, its bars the design system's `Skeleton`. It is `role="status"` with a visually hidden label, "Loading…" unless the block names what it loads; the bars stay hidden from screen readers. Six shapes: `lines` (text, a form, a set shape such as the ssh key's bar and buttons), `rows` (a list in a card), `cards` (item cards with their frame, as on the Board and Now, whose sunken surface would swallow bare bars), `tiles` (People, Projects), `table` (in place of `DataTable`'s own loading rows) and `turns` (a Thread's messages).
2. **What shows at once:** everything that doesn't wait on data, such as a card's title and note, the filter bar, the Hire a Role and Add a Project tiles. Only the part that waits gets the loader, so the page keeps its frame.
3. **Timing:** it fades in 200ms after it appears, by CSS (160ms), so a quick answer shows no flash. It shows only on the first load (`isLoading`, never `isPending`); a background refresh leaves the content alone. With reduced motion on, the bars hold still: `Skeleton` sets its shimmer inline, so that change goes into `Skeleton` itself.
4. **Empty and failed:** an empty message, or a count of 0, shows only once the data has loaded; while loading, the Board's columns and Now's headings show no count, and the chat's person picker doesn't say "No Threads yet". A failed read shows its problem where the loader was, in every block that reads it, including those that are blank for good today when a read fails: Unconfirmed hosts, Now, the chat drawer's Thread list, and every block waiting on several reads.
5. **The chat drawer** opens on the newest Thread, so it loads in the `turns` shape from the start, and the Thread's messages keep that one loader; on the Thread list it shows `rows`.
6. **Proposed for [spec 4.1](../spec/4.1-ui-rules.md)'s Words**, after "an empty block says what would fill it and how": "A block whose data hasn't arrived yet shows the shared loader, shaped like what will replace it, from 200ms on; its empty message shows only once the data has loaded, and a failed read shows its problem instead. A refresh keeps what is on screen. Small inline bits (a chip, a name, a count) show nothing until they load."

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: the loader (`BlockLoader`) and its shapes.

## The chat drawer: what it proposes

One recommendation for each of the five questions. Stanley (UI/UX Designer) was consulted and worked the Stage; their advice is folded in, and **Moved:** marks where it changed the starting position.

1. **The button** sits at the top bar's right end, after + Idea, right above where the drawer opens, so it is on every page. It has three looks: **nothing new** (its icon and "Chat"), **someone answering** (that person's avatar with a live dot) and **someone has answered** (the avatar of whoever answered, two at most, with an Ember dot). It shows a face and a dot, never a number, so it never reads as the Needs you count in the status strip. Answered wins over answering, as it is the one that waits on the user. Pressing it with an answer waiting opens that Thread; otherwise it opens where the user left off. At 600px and below it shows its mark alone. **Moved:** the answered look leads to the Thread that answered rather than only marking it.
2. **Picking a person:** with no chat open yet, the drawer opens on the people with chat on (Pam, Dwight, Michael, Angela, Jan and Stanley), each with their Role and where their chat stands: Answered, Answering… or when it last moved. Jim and Oscar, with chat off, aren't offered; a line says that people with chat off aren't listed and where chat is turned on. Once a chat is open, the person's name in the head is the switch: a menu of the same people with their dots, which switches without closing the drawer, and links to the person's page. **Moved:** the order is fixed and never sorts by who answered, so a row never moves under the pointer.
3. **Threads in a narrow drawer:** one column. The open Thread fills the drawer, in Rows, under its own row: "‹ Threads", the Thread's title and the Details switch, which serves that Thread only (spec 4.1). The head carries the line on what the person can do. In a Caretaker's Thread, the Allow changes bar sits under the Thread's row, and Allow changes opens its small window. The Thread list is a step back, with New thread at its top; each Thread shows its title, the cache dot, when it last moved and whether it answered. A person with no Threads gets an empty state that says what a Thread is, with New thread. **Moved:** "‹ Threads" carries the Ember dot when another of that person's Threads has answered, so an answer is never hidden one step back.
4. **Beside the page:** the drawer **pushes the page aside** on windows wider than 900px, with no dimmed backdrop: the page gives up the drawer's width and stays whole and usable. Over the page, it would cover the right side, which on a Task or an Initiative page is the conversation and its composer, and on Now the third panel. Dragging its left edge (or ← and → on the focused edge) resizes it between 360px and 720px, leaving the page at least 480px beside it, or 240px in view when it lies over the page, room for the conversation drawer's handle; the default is 420px, a double press on the edge goes back to it, and the width is remembered in this browser, as the rail's toggle is. **Moved:** the drawer sits below the top bar and the status strip, so the button that closes it and the Needs you count stay in reach. **Moved:** from 601px to 900px it lies over the page with no backdrop, as there is no room to push; it fills the window only at 600px and below, since at 900px a full-window chat would hide the page being asked about. Nothing scrolls sideways at any width.
5. **With everything else:**
   - The **Needs you panel** opens over the chat drawer, with its dimmed backdrop, as today: a short visit to answer and close. Beside it, 560px and 420px together would leave little page. The chat stays open under it.
   - A **window** opens over everything, side panels included, such as a Decision's window opened from a Mention in a chat.
   - On a narrow Task or Initiative page, the **conversation drawer's handle** keeps to the page's side of the chat drawer, so neither covers the other's control; the conversation drawer, opened last, lies over the chat.
   - **Esc** closes one thing per press. Drafts are kept throughout, per Thread, as today.

     | Press | What closes |
     | --- | --- |
     | 1 | A window: a Decision's, Allow changes, the Attachment viewer |
     | 2 | A menu or picker inside a panel: the person switch, the mention picker |
     | 3 | The side panel opened last: the chat drawer, the Needs you panel or the conversation drawer |
     | 4 | The next side panel, by the same rule |

     **Moved:** a menu or picker gets its own place in the order, and Esc never steps back inside the drawer, from a Thread to the list.

**This design's names**, not glossary terms: the chat drawer, the Chat button and its looks (nothing new, someone answering, someone has answered or Answered), the person picker, the person switch, the Thread list, over the page and pushes the page aside.

### What the build needs that the app doesn't have

- **A record of what the user has seen in a Thread.** A Thread is only `waiting` or `answering`, and nothing records what the user has seen, so "someone has answered" needs one: the last message seen in each Thread, set when the Thread is on screen in the open drawer. The Task page's unseen counts are kept per page view only today (`threads-seen.ts`) and could move to the same record.
- **The drawer stays open across pages.** The chat lives in the address (`?chat=…&thread=…`) today, which each navigation drops. The drawer's state (open, person, Thread, width) belongs to the shell, and a link that opens a chat, such as a Link card's or the People page's, sets it.
- **The page gives up its width.** To push the page aside, the shell's layout gives the drawer a column below the status strip, and a page folds its side column into its drawer by its own width (844px, the 900px rule less the rail) rather than the window's. That changes spec 4.1's Narrow rule, and the conversation drawer's handle anchors to the page's right edge.
- **Esc by the order things opened.** `useCloseOnEscape` finds the topmost thing by the order of `aria-modal` elements in the document. The chat drawer isn't modal, so "the side panel opened last" needs a shared record of what opened when, in `packages/ui`.
- **A resizable side panel**, a new general part for `packages/ui`, with its edge, its limits and its remembered width.
- **What changes in the spec:** spec 4's "Chats open in a modal", spec 4.1's "A chat's modal is not a window" and its Narrow rule, and the click-through's chat. The chat modal's Thread view, Allow changes bar and Thread items carry over.

## The chat flow: Chapters

The user chose Chapters from the three flows compared here. Each Thread is a box. The Thread you're on and any Thread with an open Decision stay unfolded; the others fold to one line with their Handover note, so a long Task stays short. One author's neighbouring messages join into a turn, regular messages sit on the plain surface, and your own stay neutral. Decisions, Handover notes and a failed run's line keep their colour. "Chapter" is this design's name, not a glossary term: in the app it is simply a Thread.

## Opening a page

Each page is one self-contained file, with its CSS, script and fonts inline: open it in a browser by double-click, offline. It makes no network requests.

Its colours, type and density are copied from The Office's tokens (`design-system/tokens/`, `packages/ui/src/appearance/palettes/{light,dark}.css` and `packages/ui/src/appearance/densities/comfortable.css`), so when those change, a page here keeps the values it was made with. Its tabs, buttons, badges, avatars, cards and messages are re-drawn after `packages/ui` and `apps/web/src/shell/conversation/`, so an implementation Task uses those components rather than copying this CSS. The app's themed scrollbars are in `packages/ui`. Its mock data follows the shapes of `Task`, `TaskStage`, `Thread` and `Decision` in `packages/contracts`, with a few fields for display only (people's names, messages, which Definition of Done statements are met).
