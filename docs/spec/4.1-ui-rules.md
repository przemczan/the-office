# 4.1 UI rules

How every screen is built, so the whole app reads as one. They hold wherever a click-through screen or a task doesn't say otherwise; a screen that needs to break one says why in its task. From the user's notes on the Task page, round 2 (2026-11-05), and the rules already in the code they name.

## Components

- **Use `packages/ui`.** A control that looks almost like one of its components (a button with its own border, a tab row, a select, a text field, a toggle) is that component. Only a part made for one feature, such as the Pipeline rail, is drawn on its own, and it still takes its colours, sizes and radii from the design system's tokens.
- **A new general part goes into `packages/ui`** rather than into a surface, once a second screen needs it.
- **Tokens only:** no colour, size or shadow written as a value. A Project's custom mark colour is the user's own data, so it is the one colour not taken from a token. Every screen is looked at in the Light and the Dark palettes before it is done.

## Windows

A window is the `Dialog` of `packages/ui`: something opened over the page that the user closes again. (Not the Window cap, which is a usage window.) The chat drawer is not a window: it is a side panel to work in beside the page, with no dimmed backdrop and no actions for a bar, so it keeps its own frame.

- **Three sizes**, chosen by what the window is for:
  - **Small**, 440px wide: a confirmation, such as Drop or Delete.
  - **Medium**, 720px wide: a form, such as "+ Task" or an editor.
  - **Large**, 1180px wide and up to 86% of the window's height: something to read or look at, such as the Stage history, a Decision, the Gate or the Attachment viewer.

  Each is at most 94% of the browser window's width. A window whose content changes as the user moves inside it (the Stage history) keeps one height, so it doesn't jump.
- **One frame:**
  - A **head** with what the window is (its title, or its badge and whose it is) and an X.
  - A **body** that scrolls; the head and the bar stay.
  - A **bar at the foot** with the window's actions on the left, the main one first and a destructive one last in red, and **Close** on the right, always, even when there are no other actions.
- **Closing:** Esc, the X or Close. One press of Esc closes one thing: a menu or picker first, then a window, then the side panel opened last (the chat drawer, the Needs you panel or the conversation's drawer), then the next side panel; a draft is kept throughout. Esc never steps back inside the chat drawer. A press on the dimmed backdrop never closes a window (`packages/ui/src/overlays.tsx`); the conversation's drawer on the Task and Initiative pages is the exception, a side view that keeps its draft.
- **A step before an action** (Send back's notes, a reason, a name) opens over the bar, inside the same window, and the bar's actions become the step's: its confirming action and Cancel. No window opens on top of another, except the Attachment viewer, pictures and documents alike.
- **Answering in a window is a form:** pick an option, the recommended one picked at first, or write your own words, then confirm in the bar. Outside a window, where there is nothing to look at first, one click answers.

## Layout

- **List and details:** where a list's items each have much to show (a Stage's runs), the list on the left shows each item in a line or two, and everything about the item picked shows on the right. Picking is a press on the whole item.
- **Long text** (a Handover note, a Plan, a Description) renders as Markdown at reading width, about 72 characters. Where it sits inside a stream, such as a Thread, it shows its first lines and "Read it in full", which leads to where it reads whole. A Proposal's new Office instructions have nowhere else to read whole, so the card shows them whole, scrolling in place (the user, 2026-11-06).
- **A Thread** ([spec 4](4-ux-direction.md)) sits on the plain surface at 14px, with more space between Turns than within one, and takes the whole width of the block it is in, however large the window (the user, 2026-11-05).
- **A command shows whole:** it wraps at any width, and only a very long one scrolls in its place, so no window hides part of it. The one place on one line is a fold's latest call, and the fold opens to the whole text.
- **A control that serves one tab sits on that tab's row**, as the Details switch does on a Stage run's Thread tab.
- **Pictures** show at one screenshot size, 160 by 100, wherever they are thumbnails, and open in the viewer, which steps through the pictures they were shown with.
- **The chat drawer:** on a window wider than 900px it pushes the page aside, which gives up the drawer's width and stays whole and usable; from 601px to 900px it lies over the page with no backdrop; at 600px and below it fills the window. Except when it fills the window, it sits below the top bar and the status strip. Dragging its left edge, or ← and → on the focused edge, resizes it between 360px and 720px, leaving the page at least 480px beside it or 240px in view under it; a double press goes back to 420px, and the width is remembered in this browser. A window opens over it, and so does the Needs you panel, with its dimmed backdrop.
- **Narrow:** on a page 844px wide or less (the 900px of a narrow window less the rail, so the open chat drawer counts) a page's side column becomes a drawer, whose handle keeps to the page's side of the chat drawer; nothing scrolls sideways but a row made to, such as the rail.

## Words

- UI copy follows [spec 4](4-ux-direction.md): sentence case, no "I" or "we", buttons as a verb and an object. The [glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)'s terms, exactly.
- A disabled control says why on hover; an empty block says what would fill it and how.
