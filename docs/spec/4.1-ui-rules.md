# 4.1 UI rules

Covers: components, action tones, windows, list and details, long text, Threads, commands, Attachments, the chat drawer, the Needs you panel, narrow pages, words.

- These are the rules for how every screen is built, so the whole app reads as one.
- They hold wherever a screen's [design](../design/README.md) doesn't say otherwise.
- Direction (shell, themes, brand, copy): [4](4-ux-direction.md).

## Components

- Use `packages/ui`: a control that looks almost like one of its components (a button with its own border, a tab row, a select, a text field, a toggle) is that component.
- Only a part made for one feature, such as the Pipeline rail, is drawn on its own; it still takes its colours, sizes and radii from the design system's tokens.
- A new general part goes into `packages/ui` rather than into a surface, once a second screen needs it.
- A figure against a limit reads as "41k of 200k" with a small meter; it turns amber as it nears the limit, and red only for a failure.
- Above the message input is one row: the Sub-agents and processes line from the left, the context reading at the right end, at its own width ([the design](../design/session-context.html)).
- Tokens only: no colour, size or shadow written as a value.
- A Project's custom mark colour is the user's own data, so it is the one colour not taken from a token.
- Every screen is looked at in the Light and the Dark palettes before it is done.

## Marks and their words

- Where a card shows a mark (an icon or a badge, with a count where it helps) instead of words, its words show in the `Tooltip` on hover and keyboard focus (`PlainMark`).
- Where the mark's information holds links, such as the Tasks blocking it, the mark is a button and a press opens a `Popover` anchored to it with the whole information and its links.
- A popover is not a window: no head, bar or backdrop; one is open at a time.
- A press anywhere else, Esc, a second press of its mark or following one of its links closes a popover.
- On a screen without hover, a press on any mark shows its words in a popover.

## Action tones

- An action on a record's head (a Task's, an Initiative's, an Idea's) has one tone, by what it does, so the right button is found at a glance ([the design](../design/action-colours.html), Version B).
- **Go** moves the work forward: Start, Start anyway, Resume, Mark Ready, Start Ready Tasks, Confirm the Initiative, Resume the Initiative, Shape into a Task.
- **Plan** talks it through with someone: Plan with <Planner>, Plan the Initiative, Planning Thread, Plan as a Task with <Planner>, Shape into an Initiative, and a board card's Plan.
- **Hold** pauses the work or has it wait: Pause, Pause the Initiative, Queue behind it or them.
- **Stop** stops it or takes it out: Stop, Take out of the queue.
- An action that opens or rearranges something (Open pull request, Browse files, Move up, Add a Task, Back to Shaping, Restore) has none and looks as any button.
- The colours are the status tokens: go `--success-*`, plan `--info-*`, hold `--warning-*`, stop `--danger-*`.
- A toned button takes the tone's `-subtle` surface, `-border` border and `-fg` label and icon, and the `-border` surface on hover; a ghost one only the `-fg` label and icon, with the `-subtle` surface on hover; a split button both halves.
- A primary button keeps its fill and label; its icon takes the tone's base token (`--success`, `--info`).
- The More menu shows each action's icon in its tone's `-fg`, and a board card's Plan icon too; Drop keeps the menu's danger look.

## Windows

- A window is the `Dialog` of `packages/ui`: something opened over the page that the user closes again. It is not the Window cap, which is a usage window.
- The chat drawer is not a window: it is a side panel to work in beside the page, with no dimmed backdrop and no actions for a bar, so it keeps its own frame.
- Sizes: [Defaults](#window-sizes).
- Each window is at most 94% of the browser window's width.
- Every window's top sits 7% from the top of the browser window, at least 24px, so windows line up and grow downwards.
- A window whose content changes as the user moves inside it (the Stage history, Add a Project, the HTML preview, Sub-agents and processes) keeps one height, so it doesn't jump: a large or extra-large one 86% of the browser window's height, a medium one 640px, less in a short browser window.

## Window frame

- A **head** with what the window is (its title, or its badge and whose it is) and an X.
- A preview also offers **Maximize** just left of the X: the window fills the browser window but for a 16px margin all round, and the button becomes **Restore**, which brings back the window's own size.
- Maximize isn't remembered: each window opens at its own size.
- A **body** that scrolls; the head and the bar stay.
- A **bar at the foot** with the window's actions on the left, the main one first and a destructive one last in red, and **Close** on the right, always, even when there are no other actions.

## Closing a window

- Esc, the X or Close closes a window.
- One press of Esc closes one thing: a menu, picker or popover first, then a window, then the side panel opened last (the chat drawer, the Needs you panel, the Activity panel or the conversation's drawer), then the next side panel; a draft is kept throughout.
- Esc never steps back inside the chat drawer.
- A press outside closes the topmost window or drawer: a press that starts and ends on its dimmed backdrop, the Needs you panel's included, while nothing is open over it (`packages/ui/src/overlays.tsx`).
- A drag from inside never closes it, and a menu, picker or popover open inside closes alone first.
- A window holding unsaved input (anything entered in its fields since it opened, unless the window says otherwise, as a search-only window does) first asks in a step over the bar: "Close without saving? What you entered here will be lost.", with Close without saving and Cancel.
- Cancel keeps the window, what was entered and any step it showed.
- Esc, the X and Close close that window at once.
- The conversation's drawer on the Task and Initiative pages closes on a press outside too, keeping its draft.
- The chat drawer has no backdrop and doesn't close on a press on the page.

## A step before an action

- A step before an action (Send back's notes, a reason, a name) opens over the bar, inside the same window, and the bar's actions become the step's: its confirming action and Cancel.
- No window opens on top of another, except the Attachment viewer: pictures, documents and the HTML preview alike.

## Answering in a window

- Answering in a window is a form: pick an option, the recommended one picked at first, or write your own words, then confirm in the bar.
- A Decision's form, of one or more questions, confirms with Submit or Ask me again ([decision-card.md](screens/decision-card.md)).
- Outside a window, where there is nothing to look at first, one click answers.

## List and details

- Where a list's items each have much to show (a Stage's runs), the list on the left shows each item in a line or two, and everything about the item picked shows on the right.
- Picking is a press on the whole item.
- Settings' lists (Engines, Pipelines) are `packages/ui`'s `PickList`: one card whose head names the list, its count and its main action, rows edge to edge split by hairlines, the picked one tinted with an accent bar; every such list uses it.
- Settings › Pipelines shows Pipelines and Stage templates as two tabs, each a list beside its open item ([settings.md](screens/settings.md#pipelines)).
- On a page under 1224px wide, Settings › Pipelines and a Project's Pipelines show the lists or the open item, not one under the other, the open one with a link back above it ("‹ All Pipelines", "‹ All Stage templates", "‹ the-office's Pipelines"; [the design](../design/pipeline-editor.html)).
- A control that serves one tab sits on that tab's row, as the Details switch does on a Stage run's Thread tab.

## Long text

- Long text (a Handover note, a Plan, a Description) renders as Markdown at reading width, about 72 characters.
- Where it sits inside a stream, such as a Thread, it shows its first lines and "Read it in full", which leads to where it reads whole.
- A Proposal's new Office instructions, or a prompt template's new text, have nowhere else to read whole, so the card shows them whole, scrolling in place.

## Threads and commands

- A Thread ([thread.md](screens/thread.md)) sits on the plain surface at 14px, with more space between Turns than within one, and takes the whole width of the block it is in, however large the window.
- A command shows whole: it wraps at any width, and only a very long one scrolls in its place, so no window hides part of it.
- The one place a command is on one line is a fold's latest call, and the fold opens to the whole text.

## Attachments

- Pictures show at one screenshot size, 160 by 100, wherever they are thumbnails, and open in the viewer, which steps through the pictures they were shown with.
- An HTML file opens from its chip in an extra-large window, live in a sandboxed frame, with Open the original and Download in its bar.
- An HTML file's scripts run, but it loads nothing into itself from outside the file and can't reach The Office; following a link or navigating away leaves the file, which browsers can't prevent.
- Every file Attachment can be downloaded under its own name: a document's window and the image viewer have Download, as an HTML file's preview does, and any other file downloads from its chip; a link is an address, so it has none.
- A text file opens in the document window from its chip, like a document: a document renders as Markdown, any other text file shows as plain monospace text with line numbers, both with Download.
- A text file over 1 MB shows its first 1 MB, then a line saying the rest is in the download.
- An HTML file keeps its sandboxed preview; a PDF or any other file that isn't text still downloads.

## The chat drawer

- On a window wider than 900px it pushes the page aside, which gives up the drawer's width and stays whole and usable.
- From 601px to 900px it lies over the page with no backdrop.
- At 600px and below it fills the window.
- Except when it fills the window, it sits below the top bar and the status strip.
- Dragging its left edge, or ← and → on the focused edge, resizes it between 360px and 720px, leaving the page at least 480px beside it or 240px in view under it; a double press goes back to 420px; the width is remembered in this browser.
- A window opens over it, and so does the Needs you panel, with its dimmed backdrop.

## The Needs you panel

- It lies over the page with a dimmed backdrop.
- Dragging its left edge, or ← and → on the focused edge, resizes it between 480px and 1080px, leaving at least 240px of the page in view; a double press goes back to 640px; the width is remembered in this browser.
- On a window too narrow for 480px beside that, it fills the window, with no edge.

## Narrow pages

- On a page 844px wide or less (the 900px of a narrow window less the rail, so the open chat drawer counts) a page's side column becomes a drawer, whose handle keeps to the page's side of the chat drawer.
- Nothing scrolls sideways but a row made to, such as the rail.

## Words

- UI copy follows [4](4-ux-direction.md#copy): sentence case, no "I" or "we", buttons as a verb and an object.
- The [glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)'s terms, exactly.
- A disabled control says why on hover.
- An empty block says what would fill it and how.

## Defaults

### Window sizes

- **Small**, 440px wide: a confirmation, such as Drop or Delete.
- **Medium**, 720px wide: a form, such as "New Task" or an editor.
- **Large**, 1180px wide and up to 86% of the window's height: something to read, such as the Stage history, a Decision or the Gate.
- **Extra large**, 1600px wide and up to 86% of the window's height: something to look at closely, a preview: the HTML preview, the document window and the Attachment viewer.

### Chat drawer width

- 420px after a double press; between 360px and 720px.

### Needs you panel width

- 640px after a double press; between 480px and 1080px.

### Reading width

- About 72 characters.

### Picture thumbnails

- 160 by 100.
