# 4.1 UI rules

How every screen is built, so the whole app reads as one. They hold wherever a screen's [design](../design/README.md) doesn't say otherwise.

## Components

- **Use `packages/ui`.** A control that looks almost like one of its components (a button with its own border, a tab row, a select, a text field, a toggle) is that component. Only a part made for one feature, such as the Pipeline rail, is drawn on its own, and it still takes its colours, sizes and radii from the design system's tokens.
- **A figure against a limit** reads as "41k of 200k" with a small meter; it turns amber as it nears the limit, and red only for a failure. Above the message input, one row: the Sub-agents and processes line from the left, the context reading at the right end, at its own width ([the design](../design/session-context.html)).
- **A new general part goes into `packages/ui`** rather than into a surface, once a second screen needs it.
- **Marks and their words:** where a card shows a mark (an icon or a badge, with a count where it helps) instead of words, its words show in the `Tooltip` on hover and keyboard focus (`PlainMark`). Where its information holds links, such as the Tasks blocking it, the mark is a button and a press opens a `Popover` anchored to it with the whole information and its links. A popover is not a window: no head, bar or backdrop; one is open at a time; a press anywhere else, Esc, a second press of its mark or following one of its links closes it. On a screen without hover, a press on any mark shows its words in a popover.
- **Tokens only:** no colour, size or shadow written as a value. A Project's custom mark colour is the user's own data, so it is the one colour not taken from a token. Every screen is looked at in the Light and the Dark palettes before it is done.

## Windows

A window is the `Dialog` of `packages/ui`: something opened over the page that the user closes again. (Not the Window cap, which is a usage window.) The chat drawer is not a window: it is a side panel to work in beside the page, with no dimmed backdrop and no actions for a bar, so it keeps its own frame.

- **Four sizes**, chosen by what the window is for:
  - **Small**, 440px wide: a confirmation, such as Drop or Delete.
  - **Medium**, 720px wide: a form, such as "New Task" or an editor.
  - **Large**, 1180px wide and up to 86% of the window's height: something to read, such as the Stage history, a Decision or the Gate.
  - **Extra large**, 1600px wide and up to 86% of the window's height: something to look at closely, a preview: the HTML preview, the document window and the Attachment viewer.

  Each is at most 94% of the browser window's width. Every window's top sits 7% from the top of the browser window, at least 24px, so windows line up and grow downwards. A window whose content changes as the user moves inside it (the Stage history, Add a Project, the HTML preview, Sub-agents and processes) keeps one height, so it doesn't jump: a large or extra-large one 86% of the browser window's height, a medium one 640px, less in a short browser window.
- **One frame:**
  - A **head** with what the window is (its title, or its badge and whose it is) and an X. A preview also offers **Maximize** just left of the X: the window fills the browser window but for a 16px margin all round, and the button becomes **Restore**, which brings back the window's own size. Maximize isn't remembered: each window opens at its own size.
  - A **body** that scrolls; the head and the bar stay.
  - A **bar at the foot** with the window's actions on the left, the main one first and a destructive one last in red, and **Close** on the right, always, even when there are no other actions.
- **Closing:** Esc, the X or Close. One press of Esc closes one thing: a menu, picker or popover first, then a window, then the side panel opened last (the chat drawer, the Needs you panel or the conversation's drawer), then the next side panel; a draft is kept throughout. Esc never steps back inside the chat drawer. A press outside closes the topmost window or drawer: a press that starts and ends on its dimmed backdrop, the Needs you panel's included, while nothing is open over it, so a drag from inside never closes it and a menu, picker or popover open inside closes alone first (`packages/ui/src/overlays.tsx`). A window holding unsaved input (anything entered in its fields since it opened, unless the window says otherwise, as a search-only window does) first asks in a step over the bar, "Close without saving? What you entered here will be lost.", with Close without saving and Cancel; Cancel keeps the window, what was entered and any step it showed. Esc, the X and Close close it at once. The conversation's drawer on the Task and Initiative pages closes on a press outside too, keeping its draft; the chat drawer has no backdrop and doesn't close on a press on the page.
- **A step before an action** (Send back's notes, a reason, a name) opens over the bar, inside the same window, and the bar's actions become the step's: its confirming action and Cancel. No window opens on top of another, except the Attachment viewer: pictures, documents and the HTML preview alike.
- **Answering in a window is a form:** pick an option, the recommended one picked at first, or write your own words, then confirm in the bar; a Decision's form, of one or more questions, confirms with Submit or Ask me again ([4](4-ux-direction.md)). Outside a window, where there is nothing to look at first, one click answers.

## Layout

- **List and details:** where a list's items each have much to show (a Stage's runs), the list on the left shows each item in a line or two, and everything about the item picked shows on the right. Picking is a press on the whole item. Settings' lists (Engines, Pipelines) are `packages/ui`'s `PickList`: one card whose head names the list, its count and its main action, rows edge to edge split by hairlines, the picked one tinted with an accent bar; every such list uses it. Settings › Pipelines shows Pipelines and Stage templates as two tabs, each a list beside its open item ([the design](../design/pipeline-editor.html)). On a page under 1224px wide, Settings › Pipelines and a Project's Pipelines show the lists or the open item, not one under the other, the open one with a link back above it ("‹ All Pipelines", "‹ All Stage templates", "‹ the-office's Pipelines"; [the design](../design/project-pipelines.html)).
- **Long text** (a Handover note, a Plan, a Description) renders as Markdown at reading width, about 72 characters. Where it sits inside a stream, such as a Thread, it shows its first lines and "Read it in full", which leads to where it reads whole. A Proposal's new Office instructions, or a prompt template's new text, have nowhere else to read whole, so the card shows them whole, scrolling in place.
- **A Thread** ([spec 4](4-ux-direction.md)) sits on the plain surface at 14px, with more space between Turns than within one, and takes the whole width of the block it is in, however large the window.
- **A command shows whole:** it wraps at any width, and only a very long one scrolls in its place, so no window hides part of it. The one place on one line is a fold's latest call, and the fold opens to the whole text.
- **A control that serves one tab sits on that tab's row**, as the Details switch does on a Stage run's Thread tab.
- **Pictures** show at one screenshot size, 160 by 100, wherever they are thumbnails, and open in the viewer, which steps through the pictures they were shown with.
- **An HTML file** opens from its chip in an extra-large window, live in a sandboxed frame, with Open the original and Download in its bar. Its scripts run, but it loads nothing into itself from outside the file and can't reach The Office; following a link or navigating away leaves the file, which browsers can't prevent.
- **Every file Attachment can be downloaded** under its own name: a document's window and the image viewer have Download, as an HTML file's preview does, and any other file downloads from its chip; a link is an address, so it has none.
- **A text file opens in the document window** from its chip, like a document: a document renders as Markdown, any other text file shows as plain monospace text with line numbers, both with Download. A text file over 1 MB shows its first 1 MB, then a line saying the rest is in the download. An HTML file keeps its sandboxed preview, and a PDF or any other file that isn't text still downloads.
- **The chat drawer:** on a window wider than 900px it pushes the page aside, which gives up the drawer's width and stays whole and usable; from 601px to 900px it lies over the page with no backdrop; at 600px and below it fills the window. Except when it fills the window, it sits below the top bar and the status strip. Dragging its left edge, or ← and → on the focused edge, resizes it between 360px and 720px, leaving the page at least 480px beside it or 240px in view under it; a double press goes back to 420px, and the width is remembered in this browser. A window opens over it, and so does the Needs you panel, with its dimmed backdrop.
- **The Needs you panel:** it lies over the page with a dimmed backdrop. Dragging its left edge, or ← and → on the focused edge, resizes it between 480px and 1080px, leaving at least 240px of the page in view; a double press goes back to 640px, and the width is remembered in this browser. On a window too narrow for 480px beside that, it fills the window, with no edge.
- **Narrow:** on a page 844px wide or less (the 900px of a narrow window less the rail, so the open chat drawer counts) a page's side column becomes a drawer, whose handle keeps to the page's side of the chat drawer; nothing scrolls sideways but a row made to, such as the rail.

## Words

- UI copy follows [spec 4](4-ux-direction.md): sentence case, no "I" or "we", buttons as a verb and an object. The [glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)'s terms, exactly.
- A disabled control says why on hover; an empty block says what would fill it and how.
