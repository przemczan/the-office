# 2.11.3 System health

Covers: the System health tab, its range, its blocks, its states.

## System health

- System health shows how well The Office runs, so that a slow spell can be seen and its cause found: the route or the query behind it.
- Every block is Office-wide, labelled so: the Project filter narrows none of them ([2.11.1](2.11.1-the-stats-page.md#office-wide-blocks)).
- Every chart is a line chart over the range's time axis, drawn with the design system's chart styles.
- A chart has a `Tooltip` per reading or step (on hover and keyboard focus; one tab stop, the arrow keys step, Shift for ten, Home and End).
- A chart has **Show as a table**, which gives all the block's figures, one row per reading or step.
- A gap in a line is a time nothing was read, such as The Office stopped for an update.
- The time before the first reading is hatched, "Before this start" on 1 h and "Nothing sent yet" on the longer ranges.

## The range

- 1 h (the default), 24 h, 7 d or 30 d, picked on the tabs' row and kept in the address as `?range=`, 1 h left out.
- **1 h:** each Health reading, every 5 seconds, from The Office's own readings ([5.2](5.2-stack.md), "System health"), whatever Settings › Telemetry says.
  - It is read every 5 seconds while the tab is visible.
- **24 h** by 5 minutes, **7 d** by the hour and **30 d** by 6 hours (UTC), from Grafana, as the full stats are.
  - They are read when the range changes and every minute while the tab is visible.
  - A step gives the highest event loop lag and connections waiting in it, the p95 times of all its requests and queries, and the mean of the rest.

## The source line

- It is under the tabs.
- On 1 h: "From The Office's own Health readings: one every 5 seconds since it started at 09:12, kept in memory for an hour."
- On 1 h, at its right end: "Updated 14:05:00 · every 5 seconds", with a live dot.
- On the longer ranges: "24 h, 7 d and 30 d cover what The Office has sent Grafana since it started sending these measurements."
- They hold nothing from before the release that added them.

## Load now

- Three figures, read now whatever the range.
- **Sessions running:** "Engine sessions working now: in Stages, chats and Threads."
- **Stage runs running:** "Agent and Script Stages running now, on any Task."
- **Browser connections open:** "Browser tabs following The Office's events now, from every signed-in device."

## Responsiveness

- The event loop lag over time, **p99** and **Max**, in ms.
- A dashed guide at 100 ms, "100 ms: answers feel late", shown when the scale reaches it.
- The head reads "9.1 ms p99 now · the longest wait 842 ms at 13:42:05" (on a longer range, "the highest p99" and the step's span).
- The longest wait is amber from 100 ms.
- The foot says in plain words what the lag is: how long The Office's own work waited for its turn.

## CPU and memory

- Three charts side by side, one under another on a narrow page.
- **The Office's CPU**, against one core, since The Office's own work runs on one thread: near 100%, its work queues up.
  - Its `Tooltip` gives the share of the workstation too.
- **The Office's memory:** **In use** (the process's resident memory) and **Heap used**, in MB.
- **The workstation:** its **CPU** and **Memory**, in %.
- Each chart's head gives its figure now on 1 h ("now 23% of one core"), its highest on the longer ranges.

## Requests

- **Requests a minute** and **p95 time** over time.
- The head reads "18.4k requests in the last hour · p95 46 ms".
- Under them are **the slowest routes**.
- Each route is by its method and template (`GET /api/tasks/:id/stage-runs`), never the address asked for.
- A request no route answered is one row, "Unmatched: no route answered".
- Its columns: Route, Requests, p95, Max and **Total time**, with a bar against the first row's and each route's share of all the time, "4 min 11 s 52%".

## Database

- **Queries a minute**, **p95 time** and **Waiting for a connection** (the most queries waiting for one of the pool's connections at once) over time.
- The head reads "39.2k queries in the last hour · p95 9.8 ms · up to 7 queries waited for a connection", the waiting amber when any did.
- Under them are **the slowest queries**.
- On 1 h, each **statement shape** ([5.2](5.2-stack.md), "System health"), in monospace, whole.
- On the longer ranges, by **operation and table** (`SELECT stage_runs`), as Grafana's metrics carry no shape, "Slowest queries, by operation and table".
- The foot says so on the longer ranges: "Grafana keeps queries by operation and table only, never their statements. Pick 1 h to see each statement's shape."
- The columns are as the slowest routes', with Statement shape (1 h) or Operation and table for Route, and Queries for Requests.
- On 1 h the foot says how a shape is made: "A statement's shape has its values and parameters made ?, its IN lists and VALUES rows made one ?, and is cut at 200 characters. The Office keeps the 500 shapes seen most."

## The slowest tables

- They are sorted by total time at first, what kept The Office busiest.
- Each figure column's head is a sort button (`aria-sort` on the column sorted by), largest first.
- They show the first 10 rows and **Show all 14**, then **Show the first 10**.
- Their foot says how many there are and their time in all.

## States

- **1 h always shows,** whatever the states of [Tokens and cost](2.11.2-tokens-and-cost.md#states): it reads The Office's own Health readings, never Grafana.
- **Loading:** until the first answer, each block shows its `BlockLoader` shape (`lines`, with `rows` under it for the slowest tables), and Load now its figures' places.
- A new range keeps the last answer with the blocks' loaders.
- **Couldn't read:** a read that fails puts an `Alert` above the blocks, "Couldn't read System health", then "The Office didn't answer within 10 seconds. The figures below are its last answer, from 14:04:55.", with **Try again**.
  - The blocks keep the last answer; with none yet, the Alert stands alone.
  - The source line says "Last answer 14:04:55", with no live dot.
- **Just started:** 1 h holds only the readings since The Office started, the time before hatched "Before this start", and the slowest tables only what they have seen since.
  - A block with no reading yet says "Nothing read yet. The first Health reading comes within 5 seconds of The Office starting."
  - A table with no row says "No request in the last hour. Each route shows here once The Office answers one." or "No query in the last hour. Each statement shape shows here once The Office runs one."

## States on the longer ranges

- The longer ranges follow the [states table of Tokens and cost](2.11.2-tokens-and-cost.md#states), in the same words and links, and Load now shows on every range.
- **The three Basic states:** in place of the charts, a message card with the state's words and its next step, "24 h, 7 d and 30 d read the measurements The Office sends Grafana. The last hour needs no Grafana: it comes from The Office's own Health readings.", and **Show the last hour**, which picks 1 h.
- **Grafana starting, Couldn't start, Unreachable, Read token rejected:** the state's `Alert` above, with its next step, and the same card, titled "24 h, 7 d and 30 d show once Grafana answers."
  - While Grafana starts, the charts show by themselves once it runs.
- **No data yet:** its `Alert`, "Grafana has no data from The Office yet.", then "24 h, 7 d and 30 d start with The Office's next export, within a minute.", and the card.
- **A query that fails partway** shows Unreachable's words on the blocks it fed, as in Tokens and cost.

## Defaults

### Routes are named by their template, statements by their shape

- No address, id or value reaches System health.
- A route is its method and template.
- A query is its statement shape in memory and only its operation and table on a metric ([5.2](5.2-stack.md), "System health").

### 1 h is the default range

- It is the default range of System health, and Tokens and cost keeps 7 d.
- The last hour needs nothing set up and shows a slow spell as it happens.
