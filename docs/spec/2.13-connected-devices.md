# 2.13 Connected devices

Covers: Connected device, its kinds and device key, one device to one Task, a Project's device (Project device, at rest and in use, pairing it, over USB, one environment at a time, lent to a Task), what it attaches to, the two ways to connect, the relay, the Office device helper and its start and stop, serial boards and what their port carries, the Devices section's notices, Connected device states, its lifecycle, what the run environment's image needs, the adb key, an Android phone over USB, an Android phone over wireless debugging, the run environment's network, what Agents see, who connects, the serial path, the round trip, the helper's wait, adb steps, adb reconnect, the pairing's wait, a paired phone's reconnect, the adb key's size, a dropped relay socket, the browsers supported.

## Connected device

- A **Connected device** is a device connected to one run environment, so the commands run there reach it:
  - a Task's run environment, by the user on the Task page;
  - the environment of an Action run ([2.15](2.15-actions.md)) or a test instance ([2.12](2.12-test-instances.md#install-on-a-device)) that uses one of the Project's devices, by The Office as the run starts ([A Project's device](#a-projects-device)).
- Its kinds:
  - **Android phone over USB**;
  - **Serial board over USB**;
  - **Android phone over wireless debugging**.
- Its record holds:
  - its kind;
  - its name, as the device reports it;
  - its owner: its Task, or the Action run or test instance that holds it;
  - the Project device it came from, when it did;
  - its state ([States](#states));
  - who connected it, and when;
  - once Disconnected, why, who when a user did, and when.
- Each kind's own details (what a Stage can do with it, its upload tools) are added by that kind's Tasks, following the rules on this page.
- It is never a signed-in device ([2.10](2.10-users-and-signing-in.md#signed-in-devices)), which is a browser a user signed in from.

## One device to one Task

- A device connects to one Task at a time, and to one environment at a time across The Office ([One environment at a time](#one-environment-at-a-time)).
- It is recognised by its **device key**, which each kind defines:
  - an Android phone over USB: its USB vendor, product and serial number, as the browser reports them;
  - a serial board over USB: its USB vendor and product, and an id the tab gives the port, as Web Serial reports no serial number (the system lets only one program open a port anyway);
  - an Android phone over wireless debugging: the phone's IP address, typed by the user ([Android phone over wireless debugging](#android-phone-over-wireless-debugging)).
- A device key is unique among the records not Disconnected.
- Connecting a device already connected to another Task is refused, naming that Task ([task-page.md](screens/task-page.md#devices)).
- The browser lets go of a device whose connect is refused, so other programs on the computer can open it.
- Once Disconnected, the same device can connect to any Task again, as a new record.

## A Project's device

- A device belongs to a Task, connected on it as above, or to the Project: a **Project device**, kept between uses, so an Action or a test target reaches it with nobody connecting it first.
- Any signed-in user adds one in the Project page's Devices section ([project-page.md](screens/project-page.md#devices)); it is kept until removed.
- Its kinds are a Connected device's three.
- Its record holds:
  - its kind and device key ([One device to one Task](#one-device-to-one-task));
  - its name, the user's, at most 60 characters, first the model or the name the browser reports;
  - for a phone over wireless debugging, its IP address and its Port;
  - its state at rest ([At rest and in use](#at-rest-and-in-use));
  - who added it, and when;
  - its last use: the run or Task, when, and how it ended.
- Its device key is unique among the Project's devices; the same phone may be a device of several Projects, each trusting that Project's adb key ([The adb key](#the-adb-key)).
- Agents read a Project's devices in its record (`list_projects`): each one's kind, name and state, never its address.

## At rest and in use

- **At rest**, no environment holds it, and it shows:
  - **Paired**: a phone over wireless debugging paired with the Project's adb key, whose last connect answered;
  - **Didn't answer**: a paired phone whose last connect got no answer at its address and Port; turning wireless debugging off and on changes the Port, so the user changes it, or pairs the phone again;
  - **Not paired**: a phone whose pairing failed or was cut off;
  - **Over USB**: a device over USB, remembered by its device key alone; nothing is connected at rest.
- **In use**: while an environment holds it, that environment has a Connected device record for it, made Connecting as the use starts, with the [States](#states) below; the Project device shows **In use**, naming its holder.
- The holder lets go of it at the run's end, as **run ended**, or when the Task it is lent to disconnects it; it is then at rest again, Paired or Didn't answer as that use's connect went.

## Pairing a Project device

- A phone over wireless debugging is paired once, from the Project page's Devices section, with the four values of [Android phone over wireless debugging](#android-phone-over-wireless-debugging), checked and refused by the same rules, but for those about the Task.
- The pairing runs in a **pairing environment**, started for it from the base branch's `.devcontainer/` and removed once it ends ([5.5](5.5-git-and-isolation.md#a-pairing-environment)); so it needs the Project's Run environment On.
- In it the service writes the Project's adb key, runs `adb pair`, the code on its standard input only, then `adb connect` to read the model, then `adb disconnect`, each step and the whole bounded as a Task's pairing ([The pairing's wait](#the-pairings-wait)).
- The phone then trusts the Project's adb key, so each later use only connects (`adb connect <address>:<port>`, [A paired phone's reconnect](#a-paired-phones-reconnect)), never pairs.
- Changing its Port needs no pairing: the phone trusts the key until the user removes `office@<project>` from its paired devices.

## A Project device over USB

- It is relayed from the tab that starts the run, for that run only, through [the relay](#the-relay), into the run's environment.
- The tab opens it without the browser's picker when this browser allowed it before, else the picker opens for the user to pick it.
- A device the tab can't find or open fails the run's step that needs it at once: "<name> isn't plugged into this computer, or the browser can't open it."
- The tab must stay open until the run lets go of it: closing it disconnects the device as tab closed, and that step fails.
- So a run with a Project device over USB starts only from a browser that reaches it ([Browsers supported](#browsers-supported)).

## One environment at a time

- A device, by its device key, is held by one environment at a time across The Office: a Task's device not Disconnected, or a run's hold of a Project device.
- A run whose Project device is held is refused at its start, naming the holder: "<name> is in use by <the Task's title | the Action run or test instance>."
- Connecting to a Task a device whose key is a Project device's in use is refused the same way.

## Lent to a Task

- The Task page's Devices section offers **Use a Project device** ([task-page.md](screens/task-page.md#devices)), listing the Project's devices at rest.
- The device connects to that Task's run environment as one of its Connected devices, with no pairing: a phone over wireless debugging connects with the Project's adb key, a device over USB is relayed from that tab.
- It is in use by the Task, by its record's rules here, until it is disconnected there or the Task ends; a run needing it meanwhile is refused, naming the Task.

## Removing a Project device

- Removing it is refused while it is in use, naming the holder.
- It forgets the device; its earlier uses stay on their runs and Tasks.
- The phone keeps trusting the Project's adb key until the user removes it on the phone.
- The Project's Run environment turned Off, or its Connected devices feature switched off, keeps its devices but uses and pairs none; those in use end as Run environment turned Off.

## What it attaches to

- A Task's run environment, or the environment of an Action run or a test instance holding a Project device ([5.5](5.5-git-and-isolation.md)), so a device needs the Project's Run environment On.
- Never a chat: chats run no commands ([6](6-permissions-and-safety.md#chats-only-read)).
- Never the workstation: a Task's run environment is the one place The Office runs a Project's commands kept apart from it.

## Two ways to connect

- **USB through the browser:**
  - WebUSB for an Android phone, Web Serial for a serial board;
  - in Chrome and Edge only ([Browsers supported](#browsers-supported));
  - the browser's own picker asks the user which device to connect;
  - the bytes travel through [the relay](#the-relay).
- **Android wireless debugging over the network:**
  - the user enters the phone's IP address, its port, its pairing port and its pairing code on the Task page;
  - in any browser;
  - adb in the run environment pairs with the phone and connects to it directly: no relay and no helper process;
  - the run environment must reach the phone's network ([The run environment's network](#the-run-environments-network));
  - the pairing code is used once and never kept ([Android phone over wireless debugging](#android-phone-over-wireless-debugging)).

## The relay

- For a device over USB only.
- The bytes travel: browser ⇄ the signed-in WebSocket ⇄ the service ⇄ the Office device helper in the Task's run environment.
- The **Office device helper** is The Office's own program, which the service runs in the checks' service with `compose exec -i`, its standard input and output carrying the bytes.
- For an Android phone, the helper listens on `127.0.0.1` inside the container and adb connects to it, so `adb devices` lists the phone.
- For a serial board, the helper makes a pseudo-terminal at a stable path and listens on an RFC 2217 port on `127.0.0.1` beside it ([Serial boards](#serial-boards), [RFC 2217 port](#rfc-2217-port)), both on the device's one stream: no second transport.
- No port is published, so only that run environment reaches the device ([6](6-permissions-and-safety.md#connected-devices)).
- The helper runs as the checks' user, except in its serial mode, which runs as the container's root to make the path and hands the pseudo-terminal to the checks' user.

## Serial boards

- The Office is only a bridge: the Project's own upload and monitor commands (avrdude, arduino-cli, PlatformIO…) do the upload and read the output, seeing the board at its path as if it were plugged in.
- The helper's serial mode claims the lowest free `/dev/ttyACM<n>` as a link to its pseudo-terminal ([Serial path](#serial-path)), removing links left by a helper that ended, and the link is removed as it ends.
- The pseudo-terminal belongs to the checks' user, mode 0660, so the Project's commands open it without root.
- The browser opens the board with Web Serial and the helper's frames tell it what to do, in order with the bytes:
  - **baud rate:** a tool's baud rate (and stop bits) reaches the board before the bytes written after it; the browser reopens the port at the new rate and sets DTR and RTS again;
  - **a rate the board refuses:** the browser reopens the port at the rate it had, so the board stays reachable, and the tool's next baud rate tries again; when that reopen fails too, the device ends as error, with the error;
  - **DTR and RTS:** the port's open turns them off then on, and its last close turns them off, as a real ttyACM's do, which resets DTR-reset boards (Uno, Nano, Mega, ATmega boards with a serial bootloader);
  - **reset timing:** the reset and the tool's first bytes travel in order on one channel, so the round trip slows an upload but doesn't break the reset.
- What a pseudo-terminal can't carry:
  - a tool's own DTR/RTS changes: Linux's pseudo-terminal has no modem lines, and avrdude and pyserial carry on without them;
  - data bits and parity: the pseudo-terminal is always 8 bits, no parity;
  - a board that resets into a new USB device to upload (the 1200-baud touch of Leonardo and Micro);
  - esptool's own DTR/RTS sequence for its automatic bootloader entry, which needs those lines.
- The pseudo-terminal stays for tools that take only a path; tools that set the modem lines use the [RFC 2217 port](#rfc-2217-port).
- The helper pings the browser every 5 seconds and the service keeps the round trip ([The round trip](#the-round-trip)).

## RFC 2217 port

- Beside the pseudo-terminal, the helper's serial mode serves RFC 2217 (serial over Telnet) at `rfc2217://127.0.0.1:<port>` ([RFC 2217 port number](#rfc-2217-port-number)), the fix for what the pseudo-terminal can't carry: it has no modem-line ioctls.
- esptool, pyserial and PlatformIO take it as their port (`--port rfc2217://127.0.0.1:2217`); the Project keeps its own upload commands and only points them at it.
- Through it a tool sets the baud rate, data bits, parity, stop bits, DTR and RTS, and purges; the helper answers what it can't carry (flow control, line and modem state) with fixed values, so the tool doesn't wait.
- Each baud, DTR or RTS change travels as a frame stamped with the helper's monotonic clock, in order with the bytes.
- The browser replays the changes at their stamps' offsets, reckoned from one `performance.now()` anchor, never by a chain of timers, so delays don't add up and esptool's reset keeps its 100 ms and 50 ms gaps whatever the relay's delay.
  - The first change anchors the replay; a change that comes already late runs at once and anchors the ones after it.
  - Bytes keep their place behind the changes before them.
- The connection's end turns DTR and RTS off, as a real port's close does; its open changes nothing, as the tool sets the lines itself.
- **One user at a time,** as a real port:
  - while a tool has the pseudo-terminal open, or another is connected at the RFC 2217 port, the RFC 2217 port refuses a connection and the Devices section shows the board busy notice;
  - while a tool is connected at the RFC 2217 port, opening the pseudo-terminal fails with "Device or resource busy" in the tool's own output, and no notice is shown;
  - the helper can't see a failed open without `CAP_SYS_ADMIN`, which the run environment doesn't grant, so that refusal shows no notice.
- The device's record keeps the address while Connected ([What Agents see](#what-agents-see)).

## Notices

- The Devices section says in words ([task-page.md](screens/task-page.md#devices)), and `read_task` in short words:
  - **slow round trip:** while the round trip is over 250 ms, uploads are slow;
  - **no answer after a reset:** a tool opened the port, wrote, and the board sent nothing back before the last close, so its bootloader may not wait for the round trip;
  - **1200-baud touch:** the port was closed at 1200 baud;
  - **esptool's sync unanswered:** esptool's SLIP SYNC request (`0xC0 0x00 0x08 …`) was written and its answer never came back, matched on the bytes, never on timing; once per connection;
  - **no upload tool:** neither avrdude nor arduino-cli is on the run environment's `PATH`; informational, as the Project may use another tool;
  - **board busy:** the RFC 2217 port refused a connection, as a tool had the board open at its path or another was connected there ([RFC 2217 port](#rfc-2217-port)).
- The helper's latest notice and the round trip start again at each helper start.

## The helper's start and stop

- The helper starts when a device over USB connects, or when the run environment starts or comes back after a stop while one is connected.
- It ends when the device disconnects.
- **Connecting a device never starts the run environment:** a device connected while it is stopped stays Waiting for the run environment until work starts it.
- The one exception: pairing over wireless debugging starts the Task's run environment when it isn't running, since the pairing code works once.
  - The run environment it started stops when idle as usual.
  - Connecting a paired phone again never starts it.

## States

- **Connecting**: the browser or adb is reaching the device.
- **Connected**: commands in its owner's run environment reach it.
- **Waiting for the run environment**: the device is there, but the Task's run environment isn't running; a run's hold never waits, as the run starts its environment first.
- **Disconnected**, with why:
  - **by a user**, naming them;
  - **tab closed**;
  - **Task ended**;
  - **run ended**: the Action run or test instance that held a Project device let go of it;
  - **device unplugged**;
  - **Run environment turned Off**;
  - **Connected devices switched off**;
  - **error**, with the error.
- A Disconnected record keeps why, who disconnected it when by a user, and when.
- A Disconnected record stays on the Task as its history; it never connects again.

## Lifecycle

- A Connected device belongs to the Task, not a Stage: it stays through Stage restarts and Stage ends.
- A run's hold of a Project device lasts from the run's step that needs it to the run's end, whatever way it ends; the record ends as run ended, and the Project device is at rest again ([At rest and in use](#at-rest-and-in-use)).
- A device over USB lives only while the browser tab that connected it is open; closing the tab disconnects it as tab closed.
- When the run environment stops, the idle stop included, every device shows Waiting for the run environment.
- When it starts again, each comes back: a device over USB while its tab is still open, a phone over wireless debugging while it still answers ([Android phone over wireless debugging](#android-phone-over-wireless-debugging)).
- A device never keeps a run environment running and never holds a Run environments at once slot ([5.5](5.5-git-and-isolation.md#the-other-limits-of-run-environments)).
- The Task Done or Dropped disconnects every device of it, as Task ended.
- Switching the Project's Connected devices off disconnects every open device of its Tasks, as Connected devices switched off ([Feature switches](2.1.1-projects.md#feature-switches)); connecting or pairing a device is then refused.
- Turning the Project's Run environment Off disconnects every device of its Tasks, as Run environment turned Off.
- An Office restart ([5.1.2](5.1.2-updates-and-restarts.md#restarting-the-office)) drops every relay socket:
  - a device over USB ends as tab closed, and the user connects it again;
  - a phone over wireless debugging, which has no relay socket, shows Waiting for the run environment and connects again at its next start, or ends as device unplugged, saying "pair it again", when it doesn't answer.

## What the image needs

- The Office provides the Office device helper:
  - one self-contained static Linux executable, for x64 and arm64;
  - of the release's own version;
  - mounted read-only into the run environment ([5.5](5.5-git-and-isolation.md#the-office-device-helper-in-a-run-environment));
  - no runtime needed in the image.
- The Project's `.devcontainer/` provides adb and the upload tools, such as avrdude and arduino-cli, as part of its toolchain.
- When a tool a device needs is missing from the run environment, the Devices section names it, so the user can fix the definition.

## The adb key

- One adb key per Project, made by The Office.
- Kept in the database as Secret variables are ([5.5](5.5-git-and-isolation.md#secret-variables)); never sent to the web, logged or put in a Handover note.
- Written into the run environment's `~/.android` (mode 0600) at each start.
- Both Android kinds use it, so a phone allows it once per Project.
- An RSA key of 2048 bits ([The adb key's size](#the-adb-keys-size)).

## Android phone over USB

- The browser opens the phone with WebUSB and passes its ADB packets through the relay unchanged; it doesn't run ADB itself.
- The picker lists only phones with USB debugging on.
- Its device key is `usb:<vendor>:<product>:<serial number>`; a phone that reports no serial number is refused.
- The browser opens and claims the phone before the record is made, so a phone it can't open leaves no record:
  - a phone held by adb on the user's computer is refused with "The phone is in use by adb on this computer (Android Studio or adb). Run adb kill-server, then connect again.";
  - a phone the browser may not open is refused with the udev rule (Linux) or the WinUSB driver (Windows) it needs.
- `adb devices` in the run environment lists it as `127.0.0.1:<port>`, the helper's endpoint: its adb serial.
- The service runs adb in the run environment, as the checks' user, each time the helper is ready (the first connect and every run environment start):
  - checks that adb is there, else the device ends Disconnected as error, with "adb isn't in this Project's run environment. Add Android's platform-tools to .devcontainer, then try again.";
  - writes the Project's adb key, and restarts a running adb server when the key changed;
  - runs `adb connect 127.0.0.1:<port>`, without waiting for the phone to allow it.
- When adb drops the connection while the phone is Connected (an Agent ran `adb kill-server`, say), the service connects it again ([adb reconnect](#adb-reconnect)).
- Disconnecting runs `adb disconnect` for its serial.
- The first time a Project's key reaches the phone, the phone asks to allow USB debugging; the Devices section says so until it does.

## Android phone over wireless debugging

- The user types four things from the phone's Developer options › Wireless debugging ([task-page.md](screens/task-page.md#devices)):
  - its **IP address**, an IPv4 or IPv6 literal, never a name;
  - its **Port**, the connect port the Wireless debugging screen shows;
  - its **Pairing port** and **Pairing code**, 6 digits, from "Pair device with pairing code".
- Its device key is `android-wireless:<address>`, IPv6 in its shortest lower-case form: the address alone, since the phone's ports change each time wireless debugging is turned off and on.
- Its name is the address until adb connects, then the model the phone reports (`ro.product.model`).
- Its adb serial is `<address>:<port>` (`[<address>]:<port>` for IPv6): its endpoint, kept while it is Waiting for the run environment too, so the next start knows where to connect.
- Pairing is refused, with every reason in words, unless:
  - the Task is open and its Project's Run environment On;
  - the address is one the run environment can reach: never loopback, unspecified, multicast or link-local, an IPv4-mapped IPv6 address weighed as its IPv4 one ([6](6-permissions-and-safety.md#connected-devices));
  - the Office device helper is built, as it writes the adb key.
- An open record holding the address in another Task is refused naming that Task; in this Task it is refused with "This device is already in this Task's devices. Disconnect it there first, then connect it again.", so a phone Waiting for the run environment is Disconnected before it is paired again.
- The record is made Connecting, then the service, in the background:
  - starts the Task's run environment when it isn't running ([The helper's start and stop](#the-helpers-start-and-stop)); one that can't start ends the device as error, with why;
  - checks that adb is there, else the device ends as error, with "adb isn't in this Project's run environment. Add Android's platform-tools to .devcontainer, then try again.";
  - writes the Project's adb key ([The adb key](#the-adb-key));
  - runs `adb pair <address>:<pairing port>`, the code written to adb's standard input only;
  - runs `adb connect <address>:<port>` and reads the phone's model.
- adb older than platform-tools 30.0, which has no `adb pair`, ends the device as error, with "This run environment's adb can't pair over wireless debugging: it needs Android's platform-tools 30.0 or newer in .devcontainer."
- A refused pairing ends the device as error, in adb's words; one adb paired but couldn't connect to says to check the port and pair it again.
- The whole pairing is bounded ([The pairing's wait](#the-pairings-wait)); past it the device ends as error, saying to check the address and ports and pair again with a new code.
- Paired and connected while the run environment runs, it is Connected; when the run environment stopped meanwhile, it is Waiting for the run environment.
- At each start of the run environment, a paired phone Waiting is connected again before the first check runs, never paired:
  - the adb key written, then `adb connect <address>:<port>`, bounded ([A paired phone's reconnect](#a-paired-phones-reconnect));
  - it answers: Connected;
  - it doesn't (it left the network, or wireless debugging was turned off and on, changing its port): Disconnected as device unplugged, with "The phone didn't answer at <address>:<port>; pair it again.";
  - a phone Waiting that was never paired (an Office restart cut its pairing off) ends as device unplugged, saying to pair it again.
- Disconnecting runs `adb disconnect` for its serial while the run environment runs; one disconnected while it paired is disconnected from adb again once the pairing ends.
- The Task Done or Dropped and the Run environment turned Off end it as for a device over USB ([Lifecycle](#lifecycle)).

## The run environment's network

- Each run environment has its own Compose network, an ordinary bridge with outbound NAT, so adb in it reaches a phone on the networks the host reaches.
- mDNS doesn't cross that network, so The Office never discovers a phone by mDNS; the user types its address.

## What Agents see

- `read_task` lists the Task's Connected devices: kind, name, state, and where it is reached.
  - Where it is reached, while it is Connected: the helper's endpoint, `127.0.0.1:<port>`, for an Android phone over USB, which is also its adb serial; the serial path (`/dev/ttyACM0`) and the RFC 2217 address (`rfc2217://127.0.0.1:2217`) for a serial board; `<address>:<port>`, its adb serial, for a phone over wireless debugging.
  - A serial board's line adds its baud rate, its round trip and its notices.
- A Stage's message names the devices Connected at its start, a Project device lent to the Task among them.
- An Action run's command holding a Project device gets `OFFICE_CONNECTED_DEVICES` and `ANDROID_SERIAL` by the same rules ([2.15](2.15-actions.md#what-the-office-sets)).
- Each command run through `compose exec` gets `OFFICE_CONNECTED_DEVICES` set at that run, not at the container's start, so it is never stale.
  - It is a JSON array of `{id, kind, name, endpoint, rfc2217Url}` of the Connected devices, `[]` with none; `rfc2217Url` is a serial board's RFC 2217 address, null for other kinds.
- It also gets `ANDROID_SERIAL`, that phone's adb serial, when exactly one Android phone of either kind is Connected and the Project's environment doesn't set it.

## Who connects

- Any signed-in user connects a device, from the Task page's Devices section ([task-page.md](screens/task-page.md#devices)).
- Any signed-in user sees every device of the Task and disconnects any of them.
- The record says who connected it; a run's hold says the run, and the user who started it.
- Any signed-in user adds, pairs, renames and removes a Project device, from the Project page's Devices section ([project-page.md](screens/project-page.md#devices)), and lends one to a Task from the Task page.

## Defaults

### Serial path

- A serial board's pseudo-terminal is at `/dev/ttyACM0`, or the next free `/dev/ttyACM<n>` when that one is taken.

### RFC 2217 port number

- A serial board at `/dev/ttyACM<n>` has its RFC 2217 port at `127.0.0.1:<2217 + n>`, or the next free port above when that one is taken; never published.

### The round trip

- The helper pings the browser every 5 seconds.
- A round trip over 250 ms shows the slow round trip notice.

### The helper's wait

- When the run environment starts, or a device's relay opens, the service waits at most 10 seconds for the helper to be ready.
- A device whose helper isn't ready by then ends as error.

### adb steps

- Each adb step the service runs in the run environment (the check, the key, pair, connect, the model, disconnect) is given at most 20 seconds.

### adb reconnect

- When adb drops an Android phone over USB, the service runs `adb connect` again after 2 seconds.
- At most once per 30 seconds per device.

### The pairing's wait

- Pairing over wireless debugging is given at most 30 seconds from the moment the run environment runs: adb's check, the key, pair and connect.

### A paired phone's reconnect

- At a run environment's start, a paired phone over wireless debugging has at most 10 seconds to connect again, as long as [the helper's wait](#the-helpers-wait).

### The adb key's size

- The Project's adb key is RSA, 2048 bits, named `office@<project>`.

### A dropped relay socket

- A dropped WebSocket ends the device over USB it carried as tab closed; the user connects it again.

### Browsers supported

- Connecting over USB works in Chrome and Edge, which have WebUSB and Web Serial.
- Pairing over wireless debugging works in any browser.

### Implementation notes

- Storage: `project_devices`, owned by Devices: `project_id`, its kind, device key, name, IP address and Port for a phone over wireless debugging, its state at rest, who added it and its last use; a Connected device record keeps the Project device it came from (`project_device_id`, nullable) and its holder (a Task, an Action run or a test instance).
- HTTP routes:
  - `GET /projects/:id/devices` and `POST /projects/:id/devices`, adding one;
  - `PATCH` and `DELETE /project-devices/:id`, renaming, a new Port, and removing;
  - `POST /project-devices/:id/pair`, `{address, port, pairingPort, pairingCode}`;
  - `POST /tasks/:id/devices/lend`, `{projectDeviceId}`, Use a Project device.
- Web event: `project-device.changed`, carrying the Project.
