# 2.3.2 Flows

Covers: a Pipeline as a flow, routes, routes that go back, a route's limit, the Pipeline's order, a Stage's position, the walk, which route is followed, the route forms of each Stage kind, how an Agent chooses its route, conditions, the Project-command condition and its file, when no route holds, when a limit runs out, when a Stage fails, sending a failed Script Stage back, validation, Proposals of routes, who is asked, the holder of Asked first on failures, a refused turn.

## A Pipeline is a flow

- Its Stages are the flow's nodes, joined by **routes**.
- A route leads from one Stage to any Stage of the Pipeline, an earlier one or the Stage itself included, or to the Pipeline's **End**.
- A route may carry a **condition**; a route with none always holds.
- Several routes may leave a Stage, so paths part.
- Several may lead into one, so paths meet again (a **join**).
- Only one path runs at a time: the **walk** is at one Stage at a time, and as a Stage ends it follows one route out of it.
- A Stage that joins paths runs once each time the walk arrives, never waiting for another path.
- A walk starts at the Pipeline's first Stage and ends when it follows a route to End.
- A Stage with a route to End is a **final Stage**.
- A route back to an earlier Stage, or to its own, is how the work goes round again: test-fix rounds, review-fix rounds, repair after a failed Script or Publish Stage, and Resolve after a conflict ([Conflicts in the flow](2.3.4-publish.md#conflicts-in-the-flow)).
- Going back, the walk runs the Stage again and goes on from it by its routes, every condition weighed afresh, so the path may differ from the last time.
- Conditions are data that The Office weighs itself, with no model call and no Agent, except an Agent Stage's own words, which its Agent chooses between ([How an Agent chooses its route](#how-an-agent-chooses-its-route)).
- A Stage the walk hasn't reached so far, having gone by another path, is **Not taken**: it holds no run, and the Pipeline rail shows it so.
- The walk, its routes' times and Not taken count only the runs after the Task's latest Start over ([2.4.5](2.4.5-stopping-and-restarts.md#start-over)).

## A route's limit

- Every route has its own limit, "at most N times", N from 1 to 9, or **No limit**.
- One time is the walk following that route once; the count is per walk and every route counts apart.
- A new route takes its default limit as it is drawn ([A route's limit by default](#a-routes-limit-by-default)).
- The user changes it per route in the Stage editor.
- Every loop of routes must pass through at least one route with a limit ([Validation](#validation)), so the walk always ends.
- A route at its limit that the walk would follow again stops the walk ([When a limit runs out](#when-a-limit-runs-out)); the limit never makes the walk pass over a route to the next one.
  - Only a used-up failure route passes the walk on, to the Stage's next failure route in order ([When a Stage fails](#when-a-stage-fails)).
- A Gate's Send back, a Restart from… ([2.4.5](2.4.5-stopping-and-restarts.md#restart-from)), the Definition of Done check's one more round and an answer of "Follow it once more" are no route and use none of a route's times ([2.3.3](2.3.3-gates-and-checks.md#send-back)).

## The Pipeline's order

- A Pipeline's Stages are a list, and **the Pipeline's order** is that list's order.
- It lists the Stages (the Pipeline rail, the Later Stages, the editor's selects) and says which route leads back, never which route the walk follows.
- The Pipeline's first Stage is the first in this order, where every walk starts.
- A route back is one to its own Stage or one before it in this order.
- Each Stage keeps its [position](#a-stages-position) on the flow editor's canvas, which is layout alone: moving a Stage, or Auto-align, never changes the order.
- Move earlier and Move later change it; a Stage added goes right after the picked one, or last ([its screen](screens/pipeline-flow-editor.md#moving-a-stage-in-the-order)).
- A route may lead to any Stage, its own included; the canvas draws a route back as it draws any other.
- A Stage's latest run is its last run on this Task, whichever path or lap of a loop it came by.
- A condition may name any Stage of the Pipeline, before or after the route's own in the order, and tests that Stage's latest run.
- **The previous Stage** a condition tests is the route's own Stage, the one the walk just left, whichever path it arrived by.

## A Stage's position

- Each Stage of a Pipeline, a Project Pipeline and a Task's own Stages has a **position**, `x` and `y`: where its card's top-left corner stands on the flow editor's canvas, in canvas units (a CSS pixel at 100%).
- Positions are layout alone: no rule of the walk, the [Validation](#validation), the limits or the summary reads them.
- A position is two whole numbers from −20000 to 20000; any other is refused with those words.
- End may have a position of its own; without one, it stands 76 right of the right-most Stage, level with the first final Stage in the order.
- A Stage given without a position stands 76 right of the Stage before it in the order, at that Stage's y, moved down by 16 until it covers no card; the first stands at the origin.
  - So a list with no position at all stands in one row, in its order.
  - A card is 232 wide and, for this rule, 72 high, or 82 plus 22 per route row when it lists its routes (more than one, or one with a condition or a limit).
- A Stage with a place, a column and a row, but no position (from before [the move to positions](2.3.5-library.md#the-move-to-positions)) takes the position its place gives.
- The service checks no overlap; the editor never drops a card onto another ([Moving a Stage](screens/pipeline-flow-editor.md#moving-a-stage)).
- A Stage template keeps no position.

## Which route the walk follows

- The routes out of a Stage are in an order the user sets, and the walk follows the first whose condition holds.
- A route with no condition, which always holds, therefore goes last ("otherwise"): a route after it is refused, as it could never be followed.
- At most one route leads from a Stage to any one place: two cases that lead there are one route whose condition joins them with Any of.
  - Its failure route is weighed apart, so it may lead where another of its routes leads ([When a Stage fails](#when-a-stage-fails)).
- The routes are weighed:
  - as the Stage ends done (a Gate's Continue, a Publish Stage's every result but Failed, [Publish](2.3.4-publish.md#publish));
  - as the user skips it;
  - as it fails, by its failure routes alone ([When a Stage fails](#when-a-stage-fails)).
- The routes of a Stage that needs a Decision are weighed only once the Decision is answered and the Stage has ended.
- The route followed, with its count ("2 of at most 3"), is kept on the run of the Stage it leaves and shown in its Stage history.

## The route forms

- Each Stage kind has its own form of routes out, edited in the Stage editor's Routes out ([its screen](screens/pipeline-flow-editor.md#routes-out)).
- Every row of every form has its target (a Stage or End) and its limit.
- **An Agent Stage**:
  - rows of its Agent's **choices**, each a condition in plain words for the Agent ("the review found something to fix"), followed when the Agent chooses it ([How an Agent chooses its route](#how-an-agent-chooses-its-route));
  - rows **When these hold**, with the built-in conditions below, weighed by The Office;
  - the row **When it fails**, at most one, the built-in "the previous Stage ended Failed" test on its own;
  - the row **Otherwise**, with no condition, last.
  - The choices and When these hold rows come in the order the user sets, between When it fails and Otherwise.
- **A Script Stage** and **a Gate**: rows **When these hold** with the built-in conditions, a row with "ended Failed" at its top among them for a Script Stage's failure, and an Otherwise row last.
  - A Gate never fails, so a route of a Gate testing it for Failed is refused.
- **A Publish Stage**: rows of its **results**, each one or more of Published, Nothing to publish, Conflict, After publishing failed and Failed, read "When it ends Conflict"; and an Otherwise row last ([Publish's results](2.3.4-publish.md#publishs-results)).
  - Each result leads to one place at most.
  - Otherwise takes Published and Nothing to publish only; a Conflict or After publishing failed that no row takes is asked about ([Publish's results](2.3.4-publish.md#publishs-results)).
- A Stage with one row, an Otherwise, is a straight line on.

## How an Agent chooses its route

- An Agent Stage with choice rows tells its Agent about them in its message's **Routes out** section, after the Later Stages (Prompt template `pipelines.stage-routes-out`):
  - each choice by its target's name and its words, "Review: the change touches what the user sees";
  - each route's count so far and its limit, "followed 1 of at most 3 times";
  - where the work goes otherwise, and on failure;
  - that it chooses with the `choose_route` Office tool before it ends done.
- `choose_route` takes the route, named by its target's key or `end`, and a **reason** in a sentence or two, which it must give.
- A choice that isn't one of the Stage's choice rows (a When these hold, When it fails or Otherwise row among them) is refused at once, naming the routes it may choose, so the Agent corrects it within its turn.
- It may choose again before it ends; the last choice counts, and a restart at its limits keeps it, the Handover Brief naming it.
- The choice counts only when the Stage ends done; a failed Stage follows When it fails.
- With the choice, the walk weighs the rows in order: a choice row holds when it was chosen, a When these hold row when its tests do, and Otherwise always.
- With no choice when it ends done, no choice row holds: the walk weighs the rest, so Otherwise is followed, and with none, [When no route holds](#when-no-route-holds) applies.
- Choosing a route at its limit is accepted, the tool saying the limit ran out and who will be asked ([When a limit runs out](#when-a-limit-runs-out)).
- The choice and its reason are kept on the Stage's run, shown in its Stage history ("Chose Review: the change touches what the user sees") and given to the next Agent Stage's Brief under the earlier results.
- An Agent Stage with no choice rows gets no Routes out section and no `choose_route` tool.

## Built-in conditions

- Each tests one Stage, either **the previous Stage** (the one the route leaves) or any Stage of the Pipeline named by its key and shown by its name, by that Stage's latest run:
  - it **ended Done**;
  - it **ended Failed** (a Script Stage that could not run or timed out among them);
  - it **was Skipped** ([Skipping](2.3.1-stages.md#skipping));
  - it **changed files** (its run left the Task's branch with changes against where the run started) or **changed no files**;
  - it **ran N or more times** (every run its Stage history lists for this Task, its latest included, N from 1);
  - for a Publish Stage, it **ended** with one of its results ([Publish's results](2.3.4-publish.md#publishs-results)).
- Or they test the Definition of Done: **all Reported met**, or **some not Reported met** (an Out of reach statement is not met).
- A named Stage that hasn't run on this Task holds none of the outcome or file tests and has run 0 times.
- Tests combine in groups: **All of** (AND) holds when every test or group in it holds, **Any of** (OR) when one does, and groups nest.
- The Office weighs them in the order given and stops once the result is settled, so a test after it doesn't run.

## The Project-command condition

- It is a test that names one of the Project's commands by its key (the key rules of [2.1](2.1-work.md), never `install` or `publish`), and holds when the command exits 0.
- It runs as a Script Stage's command does:
  - in the Task's worktree, through the same command runner and time limit;
  - in the run environment when it is On, after the install when its inputs changed;
  - with the Project's own variables, Secret ones included, and never The Office's GitHub token or ssh key.
- Like a test-kind command, it leaves the branch as it was.
- It gets the Task's earlier Stage runs as a JSON file whose path is in `OFFICE_STAGE_RUNS_FILE`, written outside the worktree where the command can read it (in the run environment, inside it).
- Any other exit means the test doesn't hold.
- A command that can't be weighed (the key the Project lacks, a blank line, the time limit reached, or the run environment can't start) doesn't count as not holding: the walk stops as when no route holds, the Decision saying why.
- Each run is kept on the run of the Stage the route leaves, with its command line, exit code and output, and shown in its Stage history.
- Its key counts among the commands the Pipeline runs ([A Pipeline needs the commands it runs](2.3.5-library.md#a-pipeline-needs-the-commands-it-runs)).

### The stage runs file

- It stays the one way a Project command reads how the walk went, for the Project-command condition alone; no other Stage kind needs it, since Publish's results and an Agent's choices are route conditions of their own.
- Its shape is version 2, which adds to version 1 the `publish` kind, a Publish run's `result` and an Agent's `choice`; later versions only add to it:

  ```json
  {
    "version": 2,
    "task": { "id": "6b5af1e7-…", "title": "…", "project": "the-office" },
    "route": { "from": "tests", "to": "review", "times": 1, "limit": null },
    "definitionOfDone": [
      { "number": 1, "statement": "…", "state": "reported-met" },
      { "number": 2, "statement": "…", "state": "out-of-reach", "why": "…" },
      { "number": 3, "statement": "…", "state": "not-met" }
    ],
    "stageRuns": [
      {
        "stage": "implement",
        "name": "Implement",
        "kind": "agent",
        "run": 1,
        "startedAt": "2026-11-17T10:00:00.000Z",
        "endedAt": "2026-11-17T10:40:00.000Z",
        "outcome": "done",
        "result": null,
        "choice": { "to": "tests", "reason": "…" },
        "changedFiles": ["docs/spec/2.3-pipelines.md"],
        "handoverNote": "…",
        "error": null,
        "command": null
      },
      {
        "stage": "publish",
        "name": "Publish",
        "kind": "publish",
        "run": 1,
        "startedAt": "2026-11-17T10:41:00.000Z",
        "endedAt": "2026-11-17T10:42:00.000Z",
        "outcome": "done",
        "result": "conflict",
        "choice": null,
        "changedFiles": [],
        "handoverNote": null,
        "error": null,
        "command": null
      }
    ]
  }
  ```

- `route.to` is a Stage's key, or `null` for End; `route.times` counts the times the walk followed it before, and `route.limit` is its limit, `null` for No limit.
- `stageRuns` holds every run of the Task that ended, in the order they started, the run of the Stage the route leaves last.
- `kind` is `agent`, `script`, `gate` or `publish`.
- `run` numbers the Stage's runs from 1, as its Stage history does.
- `outcome` is `done`, `skipped`, `failed`, `needs-decision` or `held` (a run a Pause or the usage window held, [A refused turn is no failure](#a-refused-turn-is-no-failure)).
- `result` is a Publish run's: `published`, `nothing-to-publish`, `conflict` or `after-publishing-failed`, else `null`.
- `choice` is the route an Agent chose with its reason, else `null`.
- `changedFiles` lists the paths the run changed in the Task's branch.
- `handoverNote` is the Agent's, else `null`.
- `error` is a failed run's.
- `command` is a Script Stage's key and exit code, the exit code `null` when the command didn't run.

## When no route holds

- It is when every route out of a Stage carries a condition and none holds, an Agent's choice rows with no choice made among them.
- The walk stops before going on, using none of a route's times.
- A Decision is put to the user directly, not the CTO first: a form of one choice.
  - The options: each route out of the Stage, by where it leads ("Go on to Review"), to follow anyway, or drop the Task.
  - Words of their own, the question's comment with no option picked, are kept for the Stage the walk goes on to, as a Gate's notes are.
- The Stage editor warns about a Stage whose routes all carry a condition, but doesn't refuse it.

## When a limit runs out

- It is when the route the walk would follow has been followed as many times as its limit allows in this walk.
- The walk stops before following it, and it is put to who is asked ([Who is asked](#who-is-asked)): the CTO first, then the user.
- The Decision names the route, its Stage and its count ("Tests has sent the work back to Implement 3 times, at most 3"), with the last run's error or Handover note.
- The options:
  - **Follow it once more**: the walk follows the route one time past its limit, and the words given guide the Stage it leads to;
  - **Go on to <target>**, for each other route out of the Stage, whatever its condition, to follow instead;
  - **Drop the Task**.
- What the CTO may answer, and why a route they let go once goes to the user the next time: [The holder of Asked first on failures](#the-holder-of-asked-first-on-failures).

## When a Stage fails

- Only its failure routes count: an Agent Stage's When it fails, a Script Stage's route whose condition says the previous Stage ended Failed (that test on its own or within an All of group at its top), a Publish Stage's Failed result.
  - A route with no condition or any other never carries a failure on.
  - The walk follows the first such route that holds and still has times left, passing over a used-up one to the next failure route in order.
- The Stage it leads to gets the failure: an Agent Stage is briefed with the error, such as a failed test run's output.
- With none left, the failure goes to who is asked ([Who is asked](#who-is-asked)), the used-up failure route named as in [When a limit runs out](#when-a-limit-runs-out), its options adding Continue with the issues.
- When they continue with the issues, the Stage's routes are weighed as when it ends done, its outcome staying Failed.
- A Publish Stage's After publishing failed is a result of its own, never a failure ([Publish's results](2.3.4-publish.md#publishs-results)).

### Sending a failed Script Stage back

- A failed Script or Publish Stage's Decision offers "Send back to "<Stage>" with these words", recommended, the Stage being the last Agent Stage the walk ran before it outside conflict handling.
- The walk goes back to that Stage, which runs with the failure's output and the words as its guidance, and goes on from it by its routes.
- Words with no option picked on its Decision mean that send-back; rerunning the Script Stage, which no Agent reads, is the explicit "Run "<Stage>" again" (or Follow it once more, when its failure route is used up), for a failure that may be flaky.
- When the walk ran no Agent Stage before it, the send-back isn't offered and the Decision says why; words of their own then run the Script Stage again.
- It is no route and uses none of a route's times, as a Gate's Send back doesn't, so the Stage failing again asks again.

## Who is asked

- Budget limits always ask the user.
- A route's limit running out, or a Stage failing with no failure route, goes first to the holder of Asked first when a Stage runs out of tries, with Consultant on.
  - They may send the Task on for one more time with guidance (a Script or Publish Stage's back to the last Agent Stage, [Sending a failed Script Stage back](#sending-a-failed-script-stage-back)), or pass it on.
  - Else it goes to the user.
- Hard switches follow their value.
- More cases: [The holder of Asked first on failures](#the-holder-of-asked-first-on-failures).

## Validation

- The Stage editor and the [Proposals](#route-and-condition-proposals) check a flow by these rules, and Save (or Apply) is refused, naming the Stage and the rule, while one fails.
- **No unreachable Stage**: a path of routes, whatever their conditions, leads from the first Stage to every Stage.
- **A route out of every non-final Stage**: every Stage has at least one route out, a final Stage's to End; a Stage left with none is refused rather than read as an end.
- **End can be reached**: a path of routes leads from every Stage to End.
- **Every loop has a limit**: each loop of routes passes through at least one route with a limit, the rule naming the loop's Stages ("Tests → Implement → Tests has no route with a limit, so it could run for ever").
- Also refused:
  - a route after one with no condition;
  - two routes from one Stage to one place, but its failure route;
  - a condition naming a Stage the Pipeline doesn't have;
  - an Agent Stage's choice row with no words, or two with the same words;
  - a Gate's route testing it for Failed;
  - a Publish result that leads to two places;
  - a limit outside 1 to 9;
  - a Project-command condition naming `install`, `publish` or a malformed key.
- A Stage whose routes all carry a condition is warned about ([When no route holds](#when-no-route-holds)).
- The Library's other flags stay as they are ([Library](2.3.5-library.md#library)).
- Going back by a Gate's Send back, a failed Script Stage's send-back or the Definition of Done check's one more round is no route.

## Route and condition Proposals

- Agents propose changes to a Pipeline's Stages, its routes and their conditions in one Proposal of its new flow, and nobody but the user applies them.
- Devops' Proposal of a Pipeline's new Stages, office-wide or a Project's ([The library editor](2.3.5-library.md#the-library-editor)), is a Proposal of its new flow: its Stages and their routes with their conditions and limits, such as "run Review only when Tests changed files or ended Failed, otherwise Publish".
  - Devops is the holder of Onboards and repairs Projects.
- The Proposal card shows each route as it was and as proposed, its condition in plain words and its limit.
- The user applies it, all or nothing, or declines it, keeping the Pipeline as it is.
- It is refused once any of those values changed since, and checked by the Validation rules above and the editor's own.
  - Positions are not among those values: what the Proposal does to them is in [2.3.5 Proposals](2.3.5-library.md#proposals).
- How the card and the editor look is [their design](../design/pipeline-flow.html).

## Defaults

### A route's limit by default

- Set as the route is drawn ([A route's limit](#a-routes-limit)).
- A route that leads back, to its own Stage or one before it in [the Pipeline's order](#the-pipelines-order): at most **3** times.
- A route that leads on, to a later Stage or End: **No limit**.

### The holder of Asked first on failures

- A Stage failing with no failure route left, and a route whose limit ran out, is put to the holder of Asked first when a Stage runs out of tries (called the CTO below) when that duty is held by a Role with Consultant on, else to the user.
- It applies to a Script or Publish Stage as well as an Agent one.
- It is The Office's Decision in a Consultation on the Task: a form of one choice, with no Ask me again ([2.4](2.4-conversations.md)).
- The CTO answers in their own words, the question's comment with no option picked, which send the Task on for one more time with their guidance, or passes it on to the user.
  - On a route whose limit ran out, or an Agent Stage's failure, that is the failed Stage's failure route, or the route whose limit ran out, followed once more, or the Stage's own round.
  - On a Script or Publish Stage's failure it is the send-back to the last Agent Stage the walk ran, when the Decision offers it ([Sending a failed Script Stage back](#sending-a-failed-script-stage-back)).
- The other options (follow once more or run it again, go on by another route or continue with the issues, drop) stay the user's, who may answer in the CTO's place.
- A guidance is the answer's words, the question's comment and the general comment, and names who gave them: the CTO, or the user, an answer given for the user at their request counting as the user's.
- A Stage or a route the CTO sent on once in a walk, a send-back to an Agent Stage included, goes to the user the next time.
- These ask the user directly:
  - the Definition of Done not reported met before Publish (or at the end of a Pipeline without one);
  - the third restart in a row during a Stage ([2.4](2.4-conversations.md));
  - a Stage in conflict handling failing with no failure route ([Conflicts in the flow](2.3.4-publish.md#conflicts-in-the-flow));
  - a Publish Stage's Conflict with no route for it;
  - budget limits.

### A refused turn is no failure

- A Stage whose turn, its first or its correction, is refused because its engine's usage window is used up doesn't fail.
- Its run ends held, as a Pause holds it ([2.1](2.1.5-planning-and-start.md#a-pause-by-the-usage-window), "A pause by the usage window").
- It follows no route and uses none of a route's times, no CTO Consultation opens and no "failed" Outcome is raised.
- The Stage history records the run as held by the usage window.
- A user's Stop still outranks it.
- **A Stand-in Engine takes the Stage on first:**
  - when a Stand-in Engine of the same engine type has room and can carry the session, the Stage goes on in the same run on it, which stays Running, and the run is held only when none has ([5.4.6](5.4.6-stand-in-engines-and-usage-windows.md#a-running-stage-switches));
  - a stand-in of another engine type never takes a running Stage, as its context would be lost, and an engine override has no stand-ins at all ([5.4.6](5.4.6-stand-in-engines-and-usage-windows.md#a-forced-engine-has-no-stand-ins));
  - the Stage history shows the switch, "Claude Code's 5-hour window was used up until 14:00, so the Stage went on on Claude Code 2";
  - a Pause, Stop or the fire alarm asked before the refusal holds the run with no switch;
  - a session over the context size set beside the list isn't switched: the Stage is held and marked as waiting for the user's choice to go on on the stand-in or at the reset.
- **The choice is one card:**
  - It is The Office's own Decision, "Decision: switch Stages to a Stand-in Engine" ([2.4](2.4.2-decisions.md#the-decision-on-stages-waiting-for-a-stand-in-engine), "The Decision on Stages waiting for a Stand-in Engine").
  - It lists every such Stage with its Task, Stage, context size, the Engine that ran out with its reset, and the Stand-in Engine it would move to now (the first of the same engine type with room, picked again on every read; "No Stand-in Engine has room now" otherwise).
  - Switch now, on a row, rechecks that the Stage still waits, moves the idle session to the first Stand-in Engine of the same engine type with room, records the stand-in on the Task and, on the held Stage run, the switch with who chose it, and resumes the Task.
  - The Task goes on in its session on the stand-in with the same model and settings, later Stages following.
  - The Stage's Brief says "It goes on on Claude Code 2, as the user Ann Lee chose", and the Stage history line ends ", as the user Ann Lee chose."
  - Each Stage is worked on its own, under the lock Work's automatic resume takes too, and one failing never blocks the others.
  - With none having room, the Stage stays paused until the reset and the card says why beside it.
  - If the move fails, the Thread stays on its own Engine and the Stage paused.
  - If resuming fails after the move, nothing is undone: the Task stays Paused, leaves the card, and the user is told the session is on the stand-in and needs Resume.
  - Stages not switched go on on their own Engine at the reset.
